> *"And without faith it is impossible to please God, because anyone who comes to him must believe that he exists and that he rewards those who earnestly seek him."*
> — Hebrews 11:6

## The One Thing That Pleases God

There are many things we assume God wants from us — obedience, sacrifice, good behavior, religious effort. And He does care about all of it. But the Bible narrows it down to one thing that stands underneath the rest: *"Without faith it is impossible to please God"* (Hebrews 11:6).

Not difficult. *Impossible.* You can do a hundred good things, but without faith, none of them reach Him. And the verse tells us why: anyone who comes to God must believe two things — that He *exists*, and that He *rewards those who earnestly seek Him*. Faith is not just believing God is real; it is believing He is good, and that seeking Him is never wasted.

So the whole Christian life rests on this word: *the righteous shall live by faith.* This morning we ask what that faith is, where it comes from, and what it looks like when it actually works.

## What Is Faith?

The clearest definition in Scripture is stunning in its confidence. *"Now faith is the assurance (the confirmation, the title deed) of the things we hope for, being the proof of things we do not see"* (Hebrews 11:1, Amplified). Faith perceives as real fact what is not yet revealed to the senses.

Notice the phrase *title deed.* A title deed is a legal document proving you own something — even if you are not standing on the property yet. That is what faith is: holding the deed to God's promise as settled fact before you ever see it with your eyes. It is not wishful thinking. It is treating what God said as *already done*.

And faith is also how we understand reality itself. *"By faith we understand that the worlds were framed by the word of God, so that what we see was not made out of things which are visible"* (Hebrews 11:3). The visible universe was spoken into being by an invisible God. Faith is simply agreeing with that upside-down truth — that the unseen is more real and more permanent than the seen.

## An Example of Amazing Faith

Jesus was rarely *amazed* by people. But one man stopped Him in His tracks. A Roman centurion sent word about his sick servant: *"Lord, don't trouble yourself, for I do not deserve to have you come under my roof... But say the word, and my servant will be healed"* (Luke 7:6b–7).

Then he explained his reasoning: *"For I myself am a man under authority, with soldiers under me. I tell this one, 'Go,' and he goes... When Jesus heard this, he was amazed at him, and turning to the crowd... said, 'I tell you, I have not found such great faith even in Israel.' Then the men who had been sent returned to the house and found the servant well"* (Luke 7:8–10).

Here is what amazed Jesus. The centurion understood *authority.* As a military man, he knew a word from someone in authority didn't require his physical presence to be obeyed. So he reasoned: if my word carries that weight, how much more does *Yours*, Lord? Just say the word. That is great faith — not a feeling, but a deep trust in the authority of Jesus' word, even from a distance.

## Where Does Faith Come From?

If faith is this important, the obvious question is: how do I get more of it? Paul answers plainly. *"Faith comes from hearing the message, and the message is heard through the word about Christ"* (Romans 10:17).

Faith is not something you manufacture by squeezing your eyes shut and trying harder to believe. It *comes* — and it comes through hearing the Word of God, specifically the word about Christ. This is why we read Scripture, why we sit under preaching, why we fill our ears with the truth. Faith grows in the soil of God's Word. Starve yourself of the Word and your faith shrinks; feed on it and your faith grows.

## Faith Needs to Be Released

Here is the heart of the sermon. Faith is not meant to sit quietly inside you. Faith is meant to be *released* — spoken, acted on, pressed toward Jesus. Watch how this plays out again and again.

Jesus taught the principle directly: *"Have faith in God. Truly I tell you, if anyone says to this mountain, 'Go, throw yourself into the sea,' and does not doubt in their heart but believes that what they say will happen, it will be done for them. Therefore I tell you, whatever you ask for in prayer, believe that you have received it, and it will be yours"* (Mark 11:22–24). Notice the action words — *says*, *asks*, *believes you have received.* Faith speaks.

Then come the stories. A blind man near Jericho heard Jesus was passing and *shouted* for mercy. The crowd told him to be quiet — *but he shouted all the more* (Luke 18:35–39). When Jesus asked what he wanted, he released it plainly: *"Lord, I want to see."* And Jesus said, *"Receive your sight; your faith has healed you"* (Luke 18:40–43). His faith refused to be silenced.

A woman who had bled for twelve years pushed through a crushing crowd just to touch the edge of Jesus' cloak — and was instantly healed (Luke 8:42–45). Jesus felt power leave Him and searched the crowd for her. Trembling, she confessed, and He said, *"Daughter, your faith has healed you. Go in peace"* (Luke 8:46–48). Her faith *reached.*

Two blind men followed Jesus crying for mercy. He asked them one question: *"Do you believe that I am able to do this?" "Yes, Lord."* Then He touched their eyes: *"According to your faith let it be done to you"* — and they saw (Matthew 9:27–30). Their sight was measured out *according to their faith.*

A Greek woman from Syrian Phoenicia fell at His feet begging for her demon-possessed daughter. When Jesus tested her with a hard word about children's bread, she answered with humble, persistent faith: *"Even the dogs under the table eat the children's crumbs."* And He said, *"For such a reply, you may go; the demon has left your daughter"* (Mark 7:24–30). Her faith *persisted* past the obstacle.

See the pattern? In every single case, faith *did something.* It shouted, it reached, it pushed through the crowd, it refused to be quiet, it persisted past a hard answer. None of these people kept their faith politely tucked away. They released it toward Jesus — and He responded every time.

## Authority to Use the Name of Jesus

Released faith operates through a specific authority: the name of Jesus. He promised, *"I will do whatever you ask in my name, so that the Father may be glorified in the Son. You may ask me for anything in my name, and I will do it"* (John 14:13–14).

The disciples took Him at His word. At the temple gate, Peter and John met a man lame from birth, begging for coins. Peter fixed his eyes on him and said, *"Look at us!"* (Acts 3:2–4). Then came one of the boldest sentences in the New Testament: *"Silver or gold I do not have, but what I do have I give you. In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, walk."* He took the man by the hand, and instantly his feet and ankles grew strong — he jumped up and went walking and leaping and praising God (Acts 3:5–8).

Peter had no money to give. But he had something far greater — authority in the name of Jesus. That same name is available to us. We do not release faith into thin air; we release it in the name that is above every name.

## We Must Live by Faith

Finally, faith is not a one-time event. It is a way of life we hold onto to the very end. *"So do not throw away your confidence; it will be richly rewarded. You need to persevere so that when you have done the will of God, you will receive what he has promised... 'But my righteous one will live by faith'"* (Hebrews 10:35–38).

Do not throw away your confidence. There will be seasons when the promise seems delayed, when the mountain does not move on your timeline, when you are tempted to give up on believing God. This is the warning and the encouragement: *persevere.* The reward comes to those who keep their faith to the end. The righteous do not just *start* by faith — they *live* by it, all the way home.

## Challenge

This week, release your faith instead of storing it.

Name the one thing you have been quietly believing God for — but only quietly. Then do what the people in these stories did: *release it.* Speak it out in prayer. Ask boldly in the name of Jesus. Press toward Him past the crowd of doubts and discouragements that tell you to be quiet. Refuse to throw away your confidence.

Feed your faith on His Word this week, because faith comes by hearing. And when the moment comes to act on what you believe — reach out, like the woman for the cloak. The righteous shall live by faith, so live by yours today.

**Without faith it is impossible to please God — so come to Him believing, and release what you believe.**
