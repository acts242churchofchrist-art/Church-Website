> *"Trust in the Lord with all your heart and lean not on your own understanding; in all your ways submit to him, and he will make your paths straight."*
> — Proverbs 3:5–6

## Back to Grade One

Think back to grade one. Before you could read a single word, you had to learn five letters that made every other letter come alive. *A, E, I, O, U.* The vowels.

Here is something we forget: you cannot form a word without them. Consonants give a word its *structure* — its bones, its shape. But the vowels are what let the word actually be *spoken*. Take the vowels out of any word and what is left cannot be said. It just sits there, silent, unpronounceable.

And that is a picture of a lot of Christian lives.

Many of us have the *structure* of Christianity down. We attend church. We know our Bible. We can say the right things at the right time. All the consonants are in place. But something essential is missing from how we actually *live* — and without it, the whole thing cannot really be spoken out loud into the world.

So today we go back to grade one. We take the five vowels — *A, E, I, O, U* — and we pair each one with a Filipino word, *Ang Limang K*, and we use them to examine one question: *ano ba talaga ang bumubuo sa buhay ng isang Kristiyano?* What actually makes up the life of a Christian?

Because it is easy to look faithful when everything is comfortable. But what happens when you are pressured, influenced, misunderstood, disappointed, confused? Each of those moments has a letter. Five tests. One life.

## A · Kilos — Action: What Pressure Reveals

The first vowel is **A — Action.** *Kilos.*

Nobody talked a bigger game than Peter. *"I will never disown you,"* he swore, *"even if all fall away."* Bold words. But the night Jesus was arrested, under real pressure, Peter denied that he even knew Him. Not once — three times (Matthew 26:31–35, 69–75).

His words said one thing. His actions revealed another. And that is the lesson of the first vowel: *kung ano ang nasa loob, lumalabas sa ilalim ng pressure.* Whatever is really inside comes out under pressure. Before the pressure, Peter had confidence — *"I will never deny You."* Under the pressure, fear — *"I do not know Him."* After the failure, repentance — *he wept bitterly.* Pressure did not *create* what was inside Peter. It *exposed* what still needed to be surrendered to God.

Jesus made the principle plain: *"Every good tree bears good fruit, but a bad tree bears bad fruit... by their fruit you will recognize them"* (Matthew 7:17–20). The fruit is the action — not the words. Anyone can say the right thing. What you actually *do*, especially when it is costly, is the fruit that reveals the tree.

But here is the grace. Jesus did not define Peter by his worst moment. On the shore, three times Peter had denied Him — and three times Jesus asked, *"Do you love Me?"* (John 21:15–17), restoring him completely. Failure exposed Peter; grace restored him. Christian action was never about flawless performance. It is about repentance and transformation. So the real question is not *"have my actions failed?"* — they have, and they will. The question is: *what will I do after God meets me there?*

## E · Kapaligiran — Environment: What Shapes You

The second vowel is **E — Environment.** *Kapaligiran.*

A paralyzed man could not get to Jesus on his own. The crowd was too thick. So four friends climbed up, tore open a roof, and lowered him down right in front of Jesus. And the text says something beautiful: Jesus saw *their* faith — the friends' faith — and healed the man (Mark 2:1–12).

The right people can carry you straight to Jesus. Your circle shapes your walk with God more than you think. *Yung barkada mo* — do they carry you closer to God, or further away?

Because your environment always has a voice. It is *always* discipling you — quietly teaching you what to desire, what to normalize, what to laugh at, what to tolerate. Your people, your places, your digital feed. *Hindi lahat ng nagtuturo sa atin ay nasa pulpito.* Not everyone who disciples you stands behind a pulpit. Sometimes it is your friend group, your workplace, or the feed you scroll before you sleep.

And do not think you are too strong to be shaped. Solomon was the *wisest* king of his generation. Yet through repeated compromise — political marriages, the slow accommodation of idolatry — his heart was gradually turned away from God (1 Kings 11:1–13). The wisest man alive drifted, not in one dramatic fall, but through a thousand small compromises. *"Do not be misled: Bad company corrupts good character"* (1 Corinthians 15:33). The same is true of the screen in your pocket: feed it what pulls you from God, and it will.

So conduct an environment audit. *Who strengthens my faith? Who makes compromise feel normal? What content keeps occupying my mind? Where am I most tempted to become someone else?* Choose people who carry you toward Jesus — and become someone who carries others to Him.

## I · Kalooban — Intention: What God Sees in the Heart

The third vowel is **I — Intention.** *Kalooban.*

Samuel went to anoint Israel's next king. He saw Eliab — tall, impressive, kingly — and thought, *surely this is the one.* But God said no. God chose David instead: the overlooked youngest son, still out in the field with the sheep, the one nobody thought to call in from work (1 Samuel 16:1–13).

Why? *"The Lord does not look at the things people look at. Man looks at the outward appearance, but the Lord looks at the heart"* (1 Samuel 16:7). *Tao tumitingin sa panlabas; ang Diyos, sa puso.* We judge one another by our actions — but God sees the *intention* behind them. That person you have quietly written off — what if God sees a heart you cannot?

This is why two identical actions can be completely different before God. Two people pray — one wants God, the other wants attention. Two people give — one gives in love, the other wants recognition. Two people serve — one serves God, the other wants control. Same act. Different heart. Only God can tell them apart (Matthew 6:1–18).

But do not hear this wrong. God is not asking you to choose between right *actions* and a right *heart.* He wants both — obedience that flows from a surrendered heart. Right action with the wrong motive is just *performance.* A good motive attached to the wrong action is just an *excuse.* But right action flowing from a surrendered heart — *that* is worship. So ask yourself the honest question: *if no one noticed, thanked, or rewarded you, would you still do it?*

## Halftime

Take a breath. Grab some water.

So far we have looked at three vowels — *how* you act, *where* you act, and *why* you act. Notice something about those three: they are all things you can, with God's help, surrender and change. You can change your actions. You can change your environment. You can surrender your intentions.

The final two vowels are different. They confront what you *cannot* control or fully understand. *Medyo iinit.* Ready?

## O · Kinalabasan — Outcome: What Actually Happens

The fourth vowel is **O — Outcome.** *Kinalabasan.*

Joseph did nothing wrong. And it cost him everything. Sold into slavery by his own brothers. Enslaved in a foreign country. Thrown into prison for *refusing* to sin. The right choice, again and again, and the outcome kept collapsing on him (Genesis 37; 39–41).

Have you ever done everything right and still watched it fall apart?

Trace his path: pit, then Potiphar's house, then prison, then — finally — the palace. Every single stage looked *disconnected* while Joseph was living through it. He could not see how the pit connected to anything good. Only much later could he look back and see that God had been working through the *whole* journey. And to the brothers who betrayed him, he said the sentence that redeems the entire story: *"You intended to harm me, but God intended it for good"* (Genesis 50:20).

Do not soften this: Joseph's suffering was *not* good. Betrayal, slavery, false accusation — those were genuinely evil. But human evil was never strong enough to cancel God's purpose. *"Many are the plans in a person's heart, but it is the Lord's purpose that prevails"* (Proverbs 19:21). And Jesus taught the same surrender: *"Whoever wants to save their life will lose it, but whoever loses their life for me will save it"* (Luke 9:24).

So when the outcome is not what you expected, God may be correcting your direction, protecting you from something unseen, developing your character, or working through timing you cannot control. You may not get a full explanation in this life. Not every *"no"* becomes a bigger *"yes"* — but every *"no"* is held by a God who is good.

This is the faith of three young men who stood before a furnace. Shadrach, Meshach, and Abednego believed God *could* rescue them. But their faith did not depend on the rescue: *"But even if He does not..."* they said — *we still will not bow* (Daniel 3:16–18). Mature faith says, *"God can."* Deeper faith says, *"Even if He does not — He is still God."*

## U · Kaunawaan — Understanding: What Only God Fully Sees

The fifth and final vowel is **U — Understanding.** *Kaunawaan.*

However bright a room is, there is still a shadow somewhere beneath. And so it is with our understanding — however clear it gets, a shadow of uncertainty always remains. Sometimes you cannot even fully explain *yourself.*

Job lost everything — his children, his wealth, his health — and he demanded to know *why.* He wanted an explanation, a point-by-point answer. And God, remarkably, never gave him one. Instead, God revealed *Himself* — His wisdom, His power, His presence — and Job trusted what he could not understand (Job 1–2; 38–42). *Can you explain everything you do? Same — hindi rin.* Neither can any of us.

Sometimes God answers the question. And sometimes God changes the one asking it.

Here is the ground we stand on when the understanding runs out: *"God is light; in him there is no darkness at all"* (1 John 1:5), the Father of lights *"with whom there is no shifting shadow"* (James 1:17). And so: *"Trust in the Lord with all your heart and lean not on your own understanding"* (Proverbs 3:5–6).

I may not know *why* this happened. I may not know *how long* it will last. I may not know *what* God will do next. But I know *who He is* — good, present, wise, and faithful. God sees what I cannot see.

## The Five Vowels

Put them together and you have the whole life:

**A — Kilos.** A Christian life is *revealed* by our actions.
**E — Kapaligiran.** It is *shaped* by our environment.
**I — Kalooban.** It is *examined* through our intentions.
**O — Kinalabasan.** It is *surrendered* in its outcomes.
**U — Kaunawaan.** It is *sustained* by trusting God beyond our understanding.

*A-E-I-O-U. Ang Limang K.* Five letters you mastered as a child. Five tests you will face as a believer.

## Jesus at the Center

But hear this clearly, because it is the most important thing said today: **this is not a five-step program for self-improvement.**

You cannot transform your actions, your environment, your intentions, your outcomes, and your understanding by willpower alone. If you walk out trying to *try harder* on five fronts, you will collapse by Tuesday. The vowels are not five things to fix. They are five places where you meet your need for Jesus — because we need Him to change the *heart* that all of it flows from.

And notice the beautiful thing about the order. *Kapag hindi mo kontrolado ang kilos ng iba, ang kapaligiran, ang kinalabasan, o hindi mo maintindihan ang nangyayari — maisusuko mo pa rin ang iyong kalooban kay Cristo.* Even when you cannot control other people's actions, your environment, or the outcome — even when you cannot understand what is happening — you can *still* surrender your heart to Christ. That one vowel, *Kalooban*, is always within reach. And it is the one that matters most.

## Challenge

This week, do not try to fix all five vowels at once. Instead, ask one question: *Aling patinig ang kinakausap sa iyo ng Diyos ngayon?* — Which vowel is God speaking to you about today?

**A · Kilos** — Is there an action that must change?
**E · Kapaligiran** — Is there an influence you must confront?
**I · Kalooban** — Is there a motive you must surrender?
**O · Kinalabasan** — Is there an outcome you are trying to control?
**U · Kaunawaan** — Is there a question you must entrust to God?

Pick the one that God just put His finger on. *Huminto. Makinig. Sagutin sa harap ng Diyos.* Stop. Listen. Answer it before God. And bring that one vowel to Jesus — not to fix by willpower, but to surrender to the One who changes the heart it all flows from.

**A Christian life, from A to U, is only ever spoken by a heart surrendered to Christ.**
