> *"The kingdom of heaven is like treasure hidden in a field. When a man found it, he hid it again, and then in his joy went and sold all he had and bought that field."*
> — Matthew 13:44

## What Would You Sell Everything For?

There is a question buried inside every parable Jesus told about the kingdom, and it is uncomfortable enough that most of us avoid answering it honestly.

*What would you sell everything for?*

Not what would you like. Not what would you save up for. What would you liquidate your entire life to obtain — house, savings, security, reputation, all of it — and walk away smiling?

Most of us would say *nothing*. And that is exactly why Jesus told these stories. Because He was describing something so valuable that a man who found it did not hesitate, did not calculate, did not weigh the pros and cons. He went, *in his joy*, and sold all he had.

Before we look at the parables, four things are worth settling about this kingdom. **Jesus' mission was to restore God's kingdom on earth.** It is **a spiritual kingdom** — the Church, not a nation with borders. **To be born again is the key** to entering it. And after His resurrection, Jesus spent **forty days** speaking about this one subject. Whatever the kingdom of heaven is, it was on His mind more than almost anything else.

## The Hidden Treasure

The first parable is one sentence long and it moves fast. *"The kingdom of heaven is like treasure hidden in a field. When a man found it, he hid it again, and then in his joy went and sold all he had and bought that field"* (Matthew 13:44).

Notice four things.

**It was a surprising discovery.** The man was not treasure hunting. He was working an ordinary field, doing ordinary labor, and he stumbled onto something extraordinary. Many people meet Christ exactly this way — not at the end of a long spiritual search, but on an ordinary Tuesday in an ordinary life.

**The kingdom has supreme value.** The man did the math instantly. Everything he owned was worth less than what was in that field. That is a real appraisal, not exaggeration. Whatever you are holding is worth less than Christ.

**His response was joy.** This is the detail we most often miss. He did not sell everything grudgingly, sighing over the cost. He sold it *in his joy*. If following Jesus feels only like loss to you, you may not have seen the treasure yet. The one who has seen it counts the sale as a bargain.

**And it required total commitment.** He sold *all* he had. Not most. Not the parts he was ready to release. All of it, to buy the one field.

## The Pearl of Great Value

Then Jesus tells it again from the opposite angle. *"Again, the kingdom of heaven is like a merchant looking for fine pearls. When he found one of great value, he went away and sold everything he had and bought it"* (Matthew 13:45–46).

The first man found the treasure by accident. This one was *searching*. He represents the person intentionally seeking God's truth — reading, asking, knocking, unsatisfied with easy answers.

And notice what he was already collecting: *fine pearls.* Not junk. Good things. The fine pearls represent everything this world offers for joy and status — career, comfort, respect, achievement. These are not evil. They are genuinely fine. That is what makes the story sting.

Because then he finds **one pearl of great value** — Christ, the gospel, the kingdom of heaven — and against that single pearl, his whole collection of fine ones becomes currency. He sells the good to obtain the best. *Selling everything* means prioritizing relationship with God above every other good thing you have gathered.

## Kingdom Conviction

So what happens when following this treasure gets hard? That is where conviction is proven — thinking, acting, and desiring in alignment with the will and glory of God.

Jesus had just given a teaching His followers found difficult. *"On hearing it, many of his disciples said, 'This is a hard teaching. Who can accept it?' Aware that his disciples were grumbling about this, Jesus said to them, 'Does this offend you? Then what if you see the Son of Man ascend to where he was before!'"* (John 6:60–62).

Notice Jesus does not soften it. He does not rush to reassure the offended crowd or trim the teaching to keep the numbers up. He lets the hard thing stand.

And people left. *"From this time many of his disciples turned back and no longer followed him. 'You do not want to leave too, do you?' Jesus asked the Twelve. Simon Peter answered him, 'Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life. We have come to believe and to know that you are the Holy One of God'"* (John 6:66–69).

That is conviction. Not "I understand everything You just said." Peter almost certainly did not. It is *"Lord, to whom shall we go?"* — the settled recognition that even when the teaching is hard, there is nowhere better to go, because this is where the words of eternal life are. Conviction is what is still standing after the crowd has walked out.

## Kingdom Priority

Conviction shows up in hard teaching. **Priority** shows up in what we actually put first. And no story in the Gospels exposes priority like the rich young ruler.

*"Just then a man came up to Jesus and asked, 'Teacher, what good thing must I do to get eternal life?' 'Why do you ask me about what is good?' Jesus replied. 'There is only One who is good. If you want to enter life, keep the commandments'"* (Matthew 19:16–17).

The man pressed for specifics, and Jesus listed them: *"You shall not murder, you shall not commit adultery, you shall not steal, you shall not give false testimony, honor your father and mother, and love your neighbor as yourself"* (Matthew 19:18–19).

And here is the remarkable part — he had kept them. *"'All these I have kept,' the young man said. 'What do I still lack?' Jesus answered, 'If you want to be perfect, go, sell your possessions and give to the poor, and you will have treasure in heaven. Then come, follow me.' When the young man heard this, he went away sad, because he had great wealth"* (Matthew 19:20–22).

Look at the phrase Jesus used: *you will have treasure in heaven.* He was offering this man the very thing the parables described — the buried treasure, the pearl of great value. And the man walked away sad, because he already had a treasure and would not sell it.

He is the exact opposite of the man in the field. Same offer. Same price. One sold everything *in his joy*; the other went away *sad*, still holding what he could not bring himself to release.

Then Jesus said the words that stunned His disciples: *"Truly I tell you, it is hard for someone who is rich to enter the kingdom of heaven. Again I tell you, it is easier for a camel to go through the eye of a needle than for someone who is rich to enter the kingdom of God." When the disciples heard this, they were greatly astonished and asked, "Who then can be saved?" Jesus looked at them and said, "With man this is impossible, but with God all things are possible"* (Matthew 19:23–26).

That last line is the hope in the whole passage. None of us can pry our own fingers off our treasure. *With God all things are possible.*

And there is reward for those who do let go. Peter, always the one to say what everyone is thinking, asked: *"We have left everything to follow you! What then will there be for us?" Jesus said to them, "Truly I tell you, at the renewal of all things, when the Son of Man sits on his glorious throne, you who have followed me will also sit on twelve thrones, judging the twelve tribes of Israel"* (Matthew 19:27–28).

And the promise widens to include every one of us: *"And everyone who has left houses or brothers or sisters or father or mother or wife or children or fields for my sake will receive a hundred times as much and will inherit eternal life"* (Matthew 19:29).

A hundred times as much. Nobody who sells everything for this treasure ends up poorer.

## The Net

But Jesus adds one more parable, and it is a sober one. *"Once again, the kingdom of heaven is like a net that was let down into the lake and caught all kinds of fish. When it was full, the fishermen pulled it up on the shore. Then they sat down and collected the good fish in baskets, but threw the bad away. This is how it will be at the end of the age. The angels will come and separate the wicked from the righteous and throw them into the blazing furnace, where there will be weeping and gnashing of teeth"* (Matthew 13:47–50).

Three things this teaches.

**The Church will attract a mixture** — both true believers and hypocrites. The net catches all kinds. You can be inside the net and not be a good fish. Attendance is not conversion.

**The sorting is done at the end, by angels.** It is not our job to pull people out of the net. The separation belongs to God, at the end of the age, and it will be exact.

**And there will be real sorrow** — intense agony and anger over the missed opportunity for salvation. Jesus does not soften this either. The grief of that day is the grief of having been so close to the treasure and never buying the field.

## A Rich Welcome

So how do we make sure we are among the good fish? Peter answers plainly. *"Therefore, my brothers and sisters, make every effort to confirm your calling and election. For if you do these things, you will never stumble, and you will receive a rich welcome into the eternal kingdom of our Lord and Savior Jesus Christ"* (2 Peter 1:10–11).

*Make every effort to confirm your calling.* Do not assume it. Do not coast on the fact that you are inside the net. Confirm it — by conviction that holds when teaching is hard, and by priority that puts the kingdom genuinely first.

And the promise on the other side is beautiful. Not a grudging admission at the gate. A *rich welcome* into the eternal kingdom. The One who was the treasure all along, welcoming home the ones who sold everything to have Him.

## Challenge

This week, name your field — and name your price.

Ask yourself honestly: *what is the one thing I am still holding that I have never actually surrendered to God?* For the rich young ruler it was money. For you it might be a relationship, an ambition, a grudge, a plan for your life, a reputation you are protecting. Whatever it is, it is the field you have not yet bought, because you will not sell what you are holding.

Then look at how you feel about the cost. If surrender feels only like loss, spend time this week looking at the treasure again — read the Gospels, sit with who Jesus actually is — until the joy comes back. The man in the field sold everything *in his joy*. That joy is available to you, and it is the surest sign you have really seen what you have found.

And make every effort to confirm your calling. Do not be a fish that was only ever in the net.

**The kingdom of heaven is a treasure — and the One who is the treasure is waiting to give you a rich welcome home.**
