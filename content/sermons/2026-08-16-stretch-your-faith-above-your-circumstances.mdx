> *"Then Jesus said to him, 'Get up! Pick up your mat and walk.' At once the man was cured; he picked up his mat and walked."*
> — John 5:8–9

## Thirty-Eight Years by the Water

There is a place in Jerusalem near the Sheep Gate called Bethesda — a pool surrounded by five covered colonnades. And in those colonnades lay a crowd of people the world had given up on. *"Here a great number of disabled people used to lie — the blind, the lame, the paralyzed"* (John 5:1–3).

Among them was one man who had been an invalid for **thirty-eight years.**

Sit with that number. Thirty-eight years of lying in the same place. Thirty-eight years of watching the water and never reaching it in time. Long enough that the mat was no longer where he slept — it was who he was. Long enough that the waiting had become a way of life.

And then Jesus walked in. *"When Jesus saw him lying there and learned that he had been in this condition for a long time, he asked him, 'Do you want to get well?'"* (John 5:5–7).

At first that sounds like a strange question. Of course he wants to get well — he has been here thirty-eight years. But Jesus never asked a careless question. This one has consequences, and it is the question this whole message turns on.

Because here is what the man answered: *"Sir, I have no one to help me into the pool when the water is stirred. While I am trying to get in, someone else goes down ahead of me."*

Notice — he did not say *yes.* He explained why it was not his fault.

Then Jesus said seven words that ended thirty-eight years: *"Get up! Pick up your mat and walk." At once the man was cured; he picked up his mat and walked* (John 5:8–11).

But the story does not end at the healing, and that matters. It was the Sabbath, and the leaders confronted him for carrying his mat. He told them the man who healed him had told him to. And then this detail: *"The man who was healed had no idea who it was, for Jesus had slipped away into the crowd."* Later Jesus found him at the temple and said, *"See, you are well again. Stop sinning or something worse may happen to you"* (John 5:12–14). And finally, *"The man went away and told the Jewish leaders that it was Jesus who had made him well"* (John 5:15).

Four movements. Let us walk through them.

## Part 1 — Be Prepared for a Change in Your Life

*"Do you want to get well?"* (John 5:6).

Why would Jesus ask that? Because healing is not free of cost. It sounds like a gift with no downside — but think about what getting well would actually mean for this man.

For thirty-eight years his entire life had been arranged around his condition. His place by the pool. His companions in the colonnade. His daily rhythm. Possibly his income, since begging was how the disabled survived. Getting well would dismantle *all of it.* He would have to find work. Find a new place. Find new friends. Be responsible for himself for the first time in nearly four decades.

That is why the question has consequences. And it is why some people, honestly, do not want to get well. The condition has become comfortable. There is a strange safety in a problem you have learned to live inside.

The same is true for us. **A Christian is a new creation in Christ Jesus** — and change in your life means change in your circumstances:

**A change of friends.** Some relationships cannot survive your obedience to Christ. Not because you become superior, but because the things you used to do together, you no longer do.

**A change in the places you go.** The places that fed the old life have to be left behind.

**A change in character.** The temper, the tongue, the habits — the new creation grows into new behavior.

**A change in responsibilities.** A healed person carries their own mat. You cannot stay dependent forever.

So Jesus is asking each of us the same thing this morning. *Do you want to get well?* Not *do you want relief* — but do you actually want the change that healing brings?

## Part 2 — Take Responsibility for Your Life

Now look again at his answer: *"Sir, I have no one to help me into the pool when the water is stirred. While I am trying to get in, someone else goes down ahead of me"* (John 5:7).

Read it carefully. He was asked a *yes or no* question, and he gave an explanation. Two explanations, actually. **No one helps me.** And **someone always beats me.**

That is the blame game. Everyone else is at fault — the people who will not help, the people who go faster. Thirty-eight years of a story about why it is not his fault.

And here is the tragedy in it: the very Person who could heal him was standing directly in front of him, and he was still talking about the pool. He was so fixed on the method he expected — the stirring water, someone to carry him — that he almost missed the Healer.

We do the same. *If only my family understood. If only I had the money. If only I had been given the chances others got. If only that person had not done what they did.* Every one of those may be true. And every one of them, held long enough, becomes a mat we lie on.

So Scripture says: **stop playing the blame game.**

**Take responsibility for your life.** Nobody else will answer for it before God.
**Stop blaming others for your condition.** Even where others genuinely wronged you, blame does not heal you.
**Learn to accept your mistakes and trust God to turn your situation around.** Owning your part is not despair — it is the doorway to God's intervention.

Notice: Jesus did not scold the man for his excuse. He simply gave a command that made the excuse irrelevant.

## Part 3 — Stretch Your Faith Beyond Your Ability

*"Then Jesus said to him, 'Get up! Pick up your mat and walk.' At once the man was cured; he picked up his mat and walked"* (John 5:8–9).

Look closely at the order, because everything is in the order.

Jesus did not heal him and *then* tell him to walk. He commanded a paralyzed man to do the one thing a paralyzed man cannot do — and the healing came *in the act of obeying.* The man had to attempt what was beyond his ability before he had the ability.

That is what it means to **stretch your faith beyond your ability.**

Faith that only attempts what is already possible is not really faith — it is planning. Real faith moves before the circumstances do. It gives before the money is there. It forgives before the feeling comes. It obeys before the outcome is guaranteed. **Faith must be accompanied by deeds.**

And sometimes God deliberately asks for the stretch. Not because He needs our effort, but because the stretching is where the faith grows.

**Our church is itself a stretch beyond our ability.** Look honestly at what we are: a congregation with limited means, volunteer leadership, and no safety net — and yet God keeps sustaining the work, the gatherings, the giving, the ministry. That is not our capacity. That is God meeting a stretched faith.

So **take effort in your faith.** Do not wait until you feel able. The man was not able. He got up anyway, and *at once* he was cured.

## Part 4 — Don't Forget God in All Circumstances

Now the strangest part of the story. *"The man who was healed had no idea who it was, for Jesus had slipped away into the crowd that was there"* (John 5:12–14).

He was healed — and he did not even know *who* had healed him. Thirty-eight years ended, and he could not name the One who ended them.

Later Jesus found him at the temple and said, *"See, you are well again. Stop sinning or something worse may happen to you."* And to his credit, *"The man went away and told the Jewish leaders that it was Jesus who had made him well"* (John 5:15). Eventually he testified. But there was a gap first — a period of walking around healed and not remembering the Healer.

That gap is a danger for every one of us. So how do we forget God?

### 1. By forgetting our past work of salvation

*"Be careful that you do not forget the Lord, who brought you out of Egypt, out of the land of slavery"* (Deuteronomy 6:12).

Israel was warned about this the moment they entered the promised land. Prosperity has a way of erasing memory. And we do it too — we forget what we were saved *from.* We forget the addiction, the emptiness, the fear, the life without Christ. **Don't forget that Jesus set you free.**

### 2. By believing the devil's lies instead of God's Word

*"This is your lot, the portion I have decreed for you," declares the Lord, "because you have forgotten me and trusted in false gods"* (Jeremiah 13:25).

Forgetting God and trusting lies are the same act. The moment we stop holding God's Word, something else fills the space. **Hide the Word in your heart so you will not sin.**

### 3. By going after other lovers

*"I will punish her for the days she burned incense to the Baals; she decked herself with rings and jewelry, and went after her lovers, but me she forgot," declares the Lord"* (Hosea 2:13).

Notice the heartbreak in that line — *but me she forgot.* God speaks like a wounded husband, because that is the relationship. **Friendship with the world is enmity against God.** When our affection goes to the world, God is not merely disobeyed; He is forgotten.

### 4. By forgetting God after receiving His blessings

*"When I fed them, they were satisfied; when they were satisfied, they became proud; then they forgot me"* (Hosea 13:6).

Read that progression: **fed → satisfied → proud → forgot.** This is the most dangerous one, because it happens after the answered prayer. The healing comes, the job comes, the provision comes — and slowly the urgency to seek God fades. We prayed hardest when we needed most.

That is exactly why Jesus went looking for the healed man afterward. The danger was not the thirty-eight years. The danger was the season *after* the miracle. **Praise God and don't forget all His benefits.**

## We Can Do Greater Things

And then Jesus gives the promise that turns this whole message into an invitation.

*"Very truly I tell you, whoever believes in me will do the works I have been doing, and they will do even greater things than these, because I am going to the Father. And I will do whatever you ask in my name, so that the Father may be glorified in the Son. You may ask me for anything in my name, and I will do it"* (John 14:12–14).

*Even greater things than these.* Not because we are greater, but because He went to the Father and gave us His name to ask in.

So the stretch is not reckless. It rests on a promise. **We can do greater things through Christ our Lord.**

## Challenge

This week, answer Jesus' question honestly: *do you want to get well?*

Name the mat. What is the condition you have been lying on so long that it has become part of who you are — a habit, a grudge, a fear, a way of thinking? And be honest about what healing would cost. New friends. New places. New character. New responsibility. **Are you prepared for that change?**

Then stop the explanation. Whatever your version of *"I have no one to help me"* is, set it down. Even where the blame is fair, it has not healed you in all these years.

Then **stretch.** Pick the one act of obedience that is currently beyond your ability and do it anyway — the giving, the forgiving, the confession, the first step back into fellowship. The man was healed *in the act of getting up*, not before it.

And when God answers — and He will — **do not forget Him.** Do not let *fed → satisfied → proud → forgot* be your story. Go find the One who healed you and tell someone what He did.

**Get up. Pick up your mat. Walk.**
