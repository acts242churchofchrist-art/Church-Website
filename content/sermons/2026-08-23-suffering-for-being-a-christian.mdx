> *"Dear friends, do not be surprised at the fiery ordeal that has come on you to test you, as though something strange were happening to you. But rejoice inasmuch as you participate in the sufferings of Christ, so that you may be overjoyed when his glory is revealed."*
> — 1 Peter 4:12–13

## A Lifetime of Preparation for One Moment

In January 2009, a passenger jet lost both engines to a bird strike minutes after takeoff from LaGuardia. There was no time to return to the airport. The captain, Chesley "Sully" Sullenberger, put the aircraft down on the surface of the Hudson River, and every single person aboard survived.

Afterward, in an interview, he said something that has stayed with a lot of people:

*"Well, I think, many people who have found themselves in such an extraordinary circumstance, they really do feel like their entire lives has been a preparation for that moment. And I think that's especially true in my case, because I remember vividly as a child knowing that I needed to be prepared for whatever might come."*

Forty years of flying. Thousands of hours of training. All of it converging on two hundred and eight seconds over New York City.

Now hold that thought against the Christian life — because Peter is writing to believers who are about to hit their own moment. And his message is the same: *this is not random, and you were not meant to be caught unprepared.*

## The Gospel in the Fire

*"Dear friends, do not be surprised at the fiery ordeal that has come on you to test you, as though something strange were happening to you. But rejoice inasmuch as you participate in the sufferings of Christ, so that you may be overjoyed when his glory is revealed. If you are insulted because of the name of Christ, you are blessed, for the Spirit of glory and of God rests on you"* (1 Peter 4:12–14).

*"If you suffer, it should not be as a murderer or thief or any other kind of criminal, or even as a meddler. However, if you suffer as a Christian, do not be ashamed, but praise God that you bear that name. For it is time for judgment to begin with God's household; and if it begins with us, what will the outcome be for those who do not obey the gospel of God?"* (1 Peter 4:15–17).

*"And, 'If it is hard for the righteous to be saved, what will become of the ungodly and the sinner?' So then, those who suffer according to God's will should commit themselves to their faithful Creator and continue to do good"* (1 Peter 4:18–19).

That is the passage. And inside it Peter gives four imperatives — four things to do when the fire comes.

## 1. Be Ready

*"Do not be surprised at the fiery ordeal that has come on you to test you, as though something strange were happening to you"* (1 Peter 4:12).

Notice what Peter assumes. Not *if* the ordeal comes — *the fiery ordeal that has come.* He is not warning about a possibility; he is addressing a present reality. And his instruction is simply: **do not be surprised.**

Why do we get surprised? Because somewhere along the way we absorbed the idea that following Jesus should make life easier. So when it makes life harder, we conclude something has gone wrong — that God is absent, or displeased, or that we misheard Him.

Peter says no. Nothing strange is happening. This is normal.

And he had already explained where the hostility comes from: *"They are surprised that you do not join them in their reckless, wild living, and they heap abuse on you"* (1 Peter 4:4).

Read that carefully, because it flips the whole thing around. **They** are the ones who are surprised. Your changed life is strange *to them*. You stopped joining the drinking, the gossip, the cutting corners, the things everyone else does without thinking — and your absence is a silent verdict on their presence. So they heap abuse on you.

That is why the mockery at work has an edge to it. That is why the family gathering gets tense when you decline. Your holiness is strange to them before their hostility is strange to you.

So **be ready.** Like Sully, know as a child of God that you need to be prepared for whatever might come. The training is not for the calm days. It is for the two hundred and eight seconds when both engines go quiet.

## 2. Be Rejoicing

The second imperative is harder, because it does not ask us merely to endure.

*"But rejoice inasmuch as you participate in the sufferings of Christ, so that you may be overjoyed when his glory is revealed"* (1 Peter 4:13).

The word is *participate.* When you suffer for the name of Jesus, you are not suffering *near* Him or *for* Him at a distance — you are participating in something He Himself went through. The insult you absorbed this week, He absorbed first. The exclusion you felt, He felt first. That is fellowship of a very costly kind, and Peter says it is grounds for joy.

And there is a specific blessing attached: *"If you are insulted because of the name of Christ, you are blessed, for the Spirit of glory and of God rests on you"* (1 Peter 4:14).

*The Spirit of glory rests on you.* Something happens in that moment. The insult is real, but so is the presence.

But Peter immediately guards against a misunderstanding: *"If you suffer, it should not be as a murderer or thief or any other kind of criminal, or even as a meddler"* (1 Peter 4:15).

Not all suffering is holy suffering. If you are suffering consequences for your own wrongdoing, that is not persecution — that is harvest. And notice the last word in that list: *meddler.* Peter puts the busybody in the same sentence as the thief. Some of us create our own opposition by being intrusive, self-righteous, or abrasive, and then call the pushback persecution. It is not. If people find you difficult, make sure it is Christ they are objecting to, and not you.

Then Jesus adds a warning that should make every believer pause: *"Woe to you when everyone speaks well of you, for that is how their ancestors treated the false prophets"* (Luke 6:26).

**Universal approval is a warning sign.** If absolutely nobody ever finds your life inconvenient, if no one has ever been made uncomfortable by what you believe, it may not mean you are winsome — it may mean you are silent. The false prophets were the popular ones. The true ones were the ones nobody invited back.

## The Church That Was Joyful in Suffering

This is exactly what we see in the early church. Look at the marks of the church in the book of Acts:

Centered on the **Savior.** Filled with the **Spirit.** Rooted in the **Scriptures.** Faithful in **Supplications.** Genuine in **Sacrifice.** Effective in **Soul-winning.** And — the one we least expect — **Joyful in Suffering.**

They were not merely surviving persecution. They were *joyful* in it. When the apostles were flogged and released, they left rejoicing that they had been counted worthy of suffering disgrace for the Name. That last mark is not a footnote to the other six; it is what proves the other six were real.

## 3. Be Resolute

*"However, if you suffer as a Christian, do not be ashamed, but praise God that you bear that name"* (1 Peter 4:16).

Shame is the enemy's most effective tool in persecution, because shame does quietly what threats cannot do openly. It makes us minimize. It makes us hedge. It makes us stay quiet in the room where speaking would cost something.

Peter's answer is not defiance — it is *praise.* **Praise God that you bear that name.** The name is not a liability you are managing. It is an honor you are carrying.

Then he widens the lens: *"For it is time for judgment to begin with God's household; and if it begins with us, what will the outcome be for those who do not obey the gospel of God? And, 'If it is hard for the righteous to be saved, what will become of the ungodly and the sinner?'"* (1 Peter 4:17–18).

This reframes everything. The suffering of God's people is a *refining* judgment — it begins with the household, and it purifies. It burns away what is not real. And if this is what refining looks like for those who *are* being saved, Peter asks, what will the reckoning be for those who never obeyed the gospel at all?

The fire you are in is a mercy. It is doing something for you that comfort never could.

## 4. Be Relying

*"So then, those who suffer according to God's will should commit themselves to their faithful Creator and continue to do good"* (1 Peter 4:19).

Two instructions, and they belong together.

**Commit yourself to your faithful Creator.** The word is the language of handing something over for safekeeping — the way you would deposit something valuable with someone you trust completely. Jesus used this same idea on the cross: *Father, into your hands I commit my spirit.* When you cannot control the outcome, you can still choose whose hands you are in.

And notice how God is described here. Not just *Creator*, but **faithful Creator.** The One who made you has not lost track of you. The same power that spoke you into being is the power holding you now.

**And continue to do good.** This is the part that costs. The natural response to being mistreated is to withdraw, to stop serving, to protect ourselves. Peter says the opposite — *keep doing good.* Keep loving. Keep serving. Keep showing up. Do not let the fire change what kind of person you are.

## Four Wrong Responses

Peter's four imperatives stand against four responses that come far more naturally.

**Resent.** Growing bitter toward the people who hurt you, or toward God for allowing it.
**Reject.** Refusing the reality — pretending it is not happening, or concluding it means God has abandoned you.
**Resign.** Giving up. Going numb. Deciding the Christian life is not worth what it costs.
**Retreat.** Withdrawing quietly — from the church, from witness, from obedience — until nothing about your life is strange to anyone anymore.

Against those four: **Be Ready. Be Rejoicing. Be Resolute. Be Relying.**

And underneath it all, one sentence worth carrying home:

*"God had one Son on earth without sin, but never one without suffering."*

If sinlessness did not exempt Jesus from suffering, nothing will exempt us. But that same truth is the comfort — because it means suffering is not evidence that something has gone wrong. The Son who suffered was the Son the Father loved.

## Challenge

This week, prepare before the moment comes.

Ask yourself honestly: **which of the four wrong responses is mine?** Do I resent, reject, resign, or retreat when following Jesus costs me something? Name it, and confess it.

Then take one concrete step in the opposite direction. If you have been **retreating** — staying quiet where you used to speak, skipping the gathering where you used to be present — go back this week. If you have been **resenting** — carrying bitterness toward a person or toward God — bring it to Him honestly and hand it over. If you have been **resigning**, ask God for the joy Peter describes: not manufactured cheerfulness, but the real joy of knowing you are participating in something Christ Himself walked through.

And do one good thing for someone who has made your faith costly. That is 1 Peter 4:19 lived out — committing yourself to a faithful Creator and continuing to do good anyway.

Sully was ready because he had spent a lifetime preparing for a moment he hoped would never come. Prepare the same way. The fire is not strange, and it is not wasted.

**If you suffer as a Christian, do not be ashamed — but praise God that you bear that name.**
