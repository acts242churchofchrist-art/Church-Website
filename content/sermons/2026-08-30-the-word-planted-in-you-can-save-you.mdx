> *"Therefore, get rid of all moral filth and the evil that is so prevalent and humbly accept the word planted in you, which can save you."*
> — James 1:21

## Something Is Already Growing in You

There is a phrase in James that most of us read too quickly: *the word planted in you.*

Not the word *preached to* you. Not the word *available to* you. **Planted in you.** James is writing to people in whom something has already been put — a living seed, placed there by God — and his instruction is not *go get it* but *humbly accept it.*

And notice what he says that planted word can do. *It can save you.*

That should stop us. We normally speak of salvation as something settled in the past — *I gave my life to Christ, I was baptized, that is done.* And that is true. But James speaks of a salvation that is still *working*, still growing, still saving. Something was planted, and it has not finished doing its work.

So this morning we ask three questions. What exactly did God make alive in us? What still has to catch up? And what does it take for that planted seed to actually grow?

## Three Parts of Human Nature

Start with what we are. *"May God himself, the God of peace, sanctify you through and through. May your whole spirit, soul and body be kept blameless at the coming of our Lord Jesus Christ"* (1 Thessalonians 5:23).

Three parts. **Spirit, soul, and body.**

Your **body** is what you can see — the physical, with its appetites and habits and aches. Your **soul** is your mind, your will, your emotions — the person you experience yourself to be. Your **spirit** is the deepest part, the part that connects with God, and it is the part that was dead before Christ.

And notice Paul's prayer: *sanctify you through and through* — all three. God is not content to save one part of you and leave the rest untouched.

This matters because of who the Word is. *"In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was with God in the beginning"* (John 1:1–2). And then: *"The Word became flesh and made his dwelling among us. We have seen his glory, the glory of the one and only Son, who came from the Father, full of grace and truth"* (John 1:14).

The Word is not merely information. **The Word is a Person.** So when James speaks of the word planted in you, he is speaking of something with the life of God in it.

## God Made Our Spirit Alive

Here is what happened at conversion. *"But because of his great love for us, God, who is rich in mercy, made us alive with Christ even when we were dead in transgressions — it is by grace you have been saved"* (Ephesians 2:4–5).

*Dead in transgressions.* That was the condition of the spirit before Christ — not sick, not weak, but dead. And God did not improve it. **He made it alive.** That is the new birth, and it is by grace, not by anything we managed.

And this is why the Word can reach places nothing else reaches: *"For the word of God is alive and active. Sharper than any double-edged sword, it penetrates even to dividing soul and spirit, joints and marrow; it judges the thoughts and attitudes of the heart"* (Hebrews 4:12).

Notice — it divides *soul and spirit.* It goes past your personality, past your preferences, past your emotions, all the way down to the deepest part. Nothing else you read this week will do that. A sermon can move your soul; the Word of God reaches your spirit.

## The Body and Soul Must Sync with the Spirit

But here is where most of us actually live. **The spirit is already new — and the mind and body have not caught up yet.**

That is the honest experience of every Christian. Something in you genuinely wants to obey God. And something else in you keeps reaching for the old habit, the old reaction, the old thought pattern. You are not a hypocrite. Your spirit is alive, and your soul and body are still being trained.

Paul tells us exactly how the catching-up happens. *"Therefore, I urge you, brothers and sisters, in view of God's mercy, to offer your bodies as a living sacrifice, holy and pleasing to God — this is your true and proper worship. Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind. Then you will be able to test and approve what God's will is — his good, pleasing and perfect will"* (Romans 12:1–2).

Two commands, one for each part. For the **body**: *offer it as a living sacrifice.* For the **soul**: *be transformed by the renewing of your mind.*

And notice the direction of the renewal. It is not renewal by effort or by willpower — it is renewal *of the mind*, and the mind is renewed by what you feed it. Which brings us back to the seed.

## We Need to Grow the Word Planted in Us

*"Therefore, get rid of all moral filth and the evil that is so prevalent and humbly accept the word planted in you, which can save you"* (James 1:21).

Two instructions again. **Get rid of** — clear the ground. **Humbly accept** — receive what has been planted. You cannot grow a crop in a field full of debris, and you cannot receive the Word with a proud heart. Humility is the posture of good soil.

And the seed is not lacking anything: *"All Scripture is God-breathed and is useful for teaching, rebuking, correcting and training in righteousness, so that the servant of God may be thoroughly equipped for every good work"* (2 Timothy 3:16–17).

*God-breathed.* The same breath that made Adam a living being is in these words. And look at the four things it does — *teaching* (here is what is true), *rebuking* (here is where you are wrong), *correcting* (here is how to get back), *training in righteousness* (here is how to walk from now on). Teaching alone informs. All four together transform.

## The Parable of the Sower

So if the seed is perfect and the sower is faithful, why do some lives change and others do not? Jesus answered that with a story.

*"That same day Jesus went out of the house and sat by the lake. Such large crowds gathered around him that he got into a boat and sat in it, while all the people stood on the shore. Then he told them many things in parables, saying: 'A farmer went out to sow his seed. As he was scattering the seed, some fell along the path, and the birds came and ate it up'"* (Matthew 13:1–4).

*"Some fell on rocky places, where it did not have much soil. It sprang up quickly, because the soil was shallow. But when the sun came up, the plants were scorched, and they withered because they had no root. Other seed fell among thorns, which grew up and choked the plants. Still other seed fell on good soil, where it produced a crop — a hundred, sixty or thirty times what was sown"* (Matthew 13:5–8).

Then Jesus explained it, and the explanation is uncomfortable, because it is a description of four kinds of hearers sitting in every church.

**The path.** *"When anyone hears the message about the kingdom and does not understand it, the evil one comes and snatches away what was sown in their heart. This is the seed sown along the path"* (Matthew 13:18–19). The soil is hard and packed down. The seed never gets in. Notice the failure point — *does not understand it.* The word is heard but never penetrates, and the enemy takes it before it can.

**The rocky ground.** *"The seed falling on rocky ground refers to someone who hears the word and at once receives it with joy. But since they have no root, they last only a short time. When trouble or persecution comes because of the word, they quickly fall away"* (Matthew 13:20–21). This is the most deceiving soil, because it looks the best at first. Immediate joy. Enthusiastic response. But there is rock underneath, so nothing goes deep — and the first real heat kills it. Not everyone who starts fast finishes.

**The thorns.** *"The seed falling among the thorns refers to someone who hears the word, but the worries of this life and the deceitfulness of wealth choke the word, making it unfruitful"* (Matthew 13:22). Here the seed grows. Nothing dramatic kills it. It is just slowly strangled by two things Jesus names precisely — *the worries of this life* and *the deceitfulness of wealth.* This is where most of us are in danger. Not rejecting the Word, just crowding it out with anxiety and ambition until it produces nothing.

**The good soil.** *"But the seed falling on good soil refers to someone who hears the word and understands it. This is the one who produces a crop, yielding a hundred, sixty or thirty times what was sown"* (Matthew 13:23).

There is the difference: *hears the word **and understands it.*** The path heard without understanding. The good soil heard *and understood* — and the harvest was thirty, sixty, a hundred times.

The seed is always good. **The difference is always the soil.**

## What Understanding the Word Actually Does

So if understanding is the difference, what does it produce? Three things.

### It Frees Us from Worldly Influence

*"To the Jews who had believed him, Jesus said, 'If you hold to my teaching, you are really my disciples. Then you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free'"* (John 8:31–32).

Read the order carefully, because we usually quote only the last line. *If you hold to my teaching* → *then you are really my disciples* → *then you will know the truth* → *then the truth will set you free.*

Freedom is at the end of a chain that begins with **holding to His teaching.** Notice Jesus said this to people who *had already believed.* Belief was the start; abiding was what made them really disciples. The truth does not set you free because you agreed with it once. It sets you free as you hold to it.

### It Frees Us from Our Sinful Nature

*"How can a young person stay on the path of purity? By living according to your word. I seek you with all my heart; do not let me stray from your commands. I have hidden your word in my heart that I might not sin against you"* (Psalm 119:9–11).

*I have hidden your word in my heart.* Not on my shelf. Not in my phone. **In my heart** — which is exactly where the seed was planted.

Because temptation does not usually arrive when you are holding a Bible. It arrives in the car, in the argument, at midnight, in the moment when there is no time to look anything up. What guards you then is what you have already hidden inside. The Word planted deep is what rises up in the moment you need it.

### Abiding in the Word Blesses Us

*"Blessed is the one who does not walk in step with the wicked or stand in the way that sinners take or sit in the company of mockers, but whose delight is in the law of the Lord, and who meditates on his law day and night. That person is like a tree planted by streams of water, which yields its fruit in season and whose leaf does not wither — whatever they do prospers"* (Psalm 1:1–3).

Notice the same planting language, but now the *person* is the tree. And notice where the tree is planted — *by streams of water.* Its root system has permanent access to what it needs, which is why it *yields fruit in season* and its *leaf does not wither.*

That is the promise for good soil. Not a life without dry seasons — but a life whose roots reach water even when the season is dry.

And notice what the blessed person does with the Word: *delights* in it and *meditates* on it day and night. That is more than reading. Delight means you actually want it. Meditation means you keep turning it over. That is how a seed becomes a tree.

## Challenge

This week, work on your soil.

**Ask honestly which soil you are.** Is the Word bouncing off hard ground because you hear it without ever really understanding it? Are you rocky — quick joy, no root, first to fold when things get hard? Or are you thorny, where the seed is genuinely growing but the worries of this life and the pull of money are slowly choking it? Be honest. Most of us know which one we are.

**Then clear one thing.** James says *get rid of* before *humbly accept.* Name one thorn — one worry you keep feeding, one ambition that has quietly taken over — and bring it to God this week.

**And hide one verse in your heart.** Not read it. *Hide* it — memorize it, carry it, turn it over during the day until it is planted deep enough to rise up on its own when you need it.

The seed is already in you. The sower has already been faithful. What remains is the soil.

**Humbly accept the word planted in you — it can save you.**
