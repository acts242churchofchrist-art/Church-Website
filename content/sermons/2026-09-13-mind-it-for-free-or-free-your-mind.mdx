> *"You will keep in perfect peace those whose minds are steadfast, because they trust in you."*
> — Isaiah 26:3

## What Has Been Living Rent-Free in Your Mind?

Take a moment with the question before you answer it. *Ano ang nakatira sa isip mo nang walang upa?*

Maybe it is a message you keep rereading. A bill you cannot stop calculating. A person. A look someone gave you that you have replayed a hundred times since. Something moved into your mind this week and it has not paid a single peso of rent — and you have let it stay.

This message continues our series on the vowels of a Christian life, and we return to **U · Kaunawaan**, understanding. Remember what we said: **however clear our understanding gets, a shadow of uncertainty always remains.** However bright the room, there is still a shadow beneath you.

And here is the God we stand under while we live in that shadow: *"Every good and perfect gift is from above, coming down from the Father of the heavenly lights, who does not change like shifting shadows"* (James 1:17). **There is no shadow in Him.** No shifting. No uncertainty.

So the question today is not how to escape the shadow. We will all stand in it. The question is: **while we stand there, what are we letting our mind hold?**

## A Free Mind Is Not an Empty Mind

Let us define what we are after. **A free mind is free from distractions, sin, and doubt. It is grounded in the Word of God, and it does not falter.**

Notice what that is not. **A free mind is not an empty mind. It is a steadfast mind.** Emptying your head is not the goal — plenty of people are trying that, and all it does is make room for something worse to move in. What we want is a mind so anchored that the noise cannot move it.

*"You will keep in perfect peace those whose minds are steadfast, because they trust in you"* (Isaiah 26:3).

**Perfect peace is promised to a steadfast mind.** Not to a clever one, not to an untroubled one — a steadfast one, held in place by trust.

So every day we face a choice between two ways of holding a thought.

**Mind it for free:** let it stay rent-free. Feed it until it grows. Miss what is right in front of you.

**Free your mind:** let it go. Take it captive. See what God has actually placed in front of you.

We will make that choice in every section this morning. *Mind it for free?* — **Free your mind!**

## 1 · The Brewing — Temptation

*Ang Tukso.* And the question here is simple: **can one look be enough?** *Isang tingin, enough na ba?*

Look at how it began for David. The chapter opens by telling us that in the spring, when kings went off to war, **David remained in Jerusalem.** He stayed. And an idle mind has room to rent out.

*"One evening David got up from his bed and walked around on the roof of the palace. From the roof he saw a woman bathing. The woman was very beautiful, and David sent someone to find out about her. The man said, 'She is Bathsheba, the daughter of Eliam and the wife of Uriah the Hittite'"* (2 Samuel 11:2–3).

Watch the sequence, because it is the whole anatomy of a fall. **He stayed. He saw. He asked. He sent.**

The seeing was the encounter — that happens to all of us, and it was not yet the sin. **The asking was the minding.** That was the moment the thought was invited to stay. And then verse 4: *"Then David sent messengers to get her."* One look, minded for free, became full-grown sin.

James maps the same progression: *"But each person is tempted when they are dragged away by their own evil desire and enticed. Then, after desire has conceived, it gives birth to sin; and sin, when it is full-grown, gives birth to death"* (James 1:14–15).

Notice the biology in that verse. **Conceived. Gives birth. Full-grown.** Nothing arrives fully formed. Everything that destroys a person started as something small that was allowed to gestate.

So hear this clearly: **being tempted is not yet sin.** The thought that arrives uninvited is not your guilt. **But a temptation you keep minding is a temptation you keep feeding.**

And what do we do instead? *"We demolish arguments and every pretension that sets itself up against the knowledge of God, and we take captive every thought to make it obedient to Christ"* (2 Corinthians 10:5).

**Don't negotiate with the thought. Take it captive.** The moment you start reasoning with it — *just this once, it is not that serious, I can handle it* — you have already given it a lease.

*Mind it for free?* — **Free your mind!**

## 2 · The Multiplying — Worries and Fears

*Pag-aalala at Takot.* And the honest question: **has worrying ever solved anything?**

*"Therefore do not worry about tomorrow, for tomorrow will worry about itself. Each day has enough trouble of its own"* (Matthew 6:34).

Here is what worry actually does. **Minding it doesn't fix it. It only multiplies it.** *Isang problema, pero sa kakaisip, nagiging sampu.* One problem goes in, and by the time you have turned it over all night, you are carrying ten — nine of which have not happened and may never happen.

So what do we do with it? *"Do not be anxious about anything, but in every situation, by prayer and petition, with thanksgiving, present your requests to God. And the peace of God, which transcends all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus"* (Philippians 4:6–7).

Look carefully at that phrase: **the peace of God transcends all understanding.** This is exactly where our series lands. **Peace does not wait for full understanding.** You do not have to figure it out before you are allowed to be at peace. The peace comes over the top of the not-knowing, and stands guard over your heart and your mind.

And the season itself has a limit: *"For his anger lasts only a moment, but his favor lasts a lifetime; weeping may stay for the night, but rejoicing comes in the morning"* (Psalm 30:5).

**Weeping may stay for the night.** It is real, and it is allowed, and it is temporary. **But rejoicing comes in the morning.**

So: **let go and trust God in our walk. The night will end. Don't stretch it by minding it.** Because that is what minding does to a night — it makes it longer than God intended it to be.

*Mind it for free?* — **Free your mind!**

## 3 · Two Sons, Two Minds

*Dalawang Anak, Dalawang Isip.* We know this parable well — but let us not read it for the events this time. **Let us read it for the relationships.** Same father. Two sons. Two minds.

### The Younger Son

*"The younger one said to his father, 'Father, give me my share of the estate.' So he divided his property between them. Not long after that, the younger son got together all he had, set off for a distant country and there squandered his wealth in wild living"* (Luke 15:12–13).

**He minded the inheritance until it moved him.** He was not in a far country yet when he was lost — the leaving happened in his mind long before his feet crossed the border. Free hands, captive mind.

And this is where the world's version of freedom needs naming. **The world says: *my life, my choice, don't judge me.*** Our culture puts enormous weight on freedom — especially freedom from judgment. And it creates an illusion: *if no one can judge me, then nothing will follow me.*

But hear this carefully — and this is not a call to judge anyone. **Freedom from judgment is not freedom from consequences.** *Walang judgment? Maybe. Walang consequences? Hindi.*

*"Do not be deceived: God cannot be mocked. A man reaps what he sows"* (Galatians 6:7).

Follow what happens next in the story. He spent everything. Then a famine came. Then he was feeding pigs. Then — the loneliest line in the parable — **no one gave him anything.** Every *my way* was quietly adding up. And at the end he was left holding all of it at once: the sins, the problems, the chains, aggregated.

**We often do exactly what the younger son did.**

*"When he came to his senses, he said, 'How many of my father's hired servants have food to spare, and here I am starving to death!'... So he got up and went to his father. But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and was filled with compassion for him; he ran to his son, threw his arms around him and kissed him"* (Luke 15:17, 20).

**When he came to his senses.** The turning happened in his mind first. **His mind came home before his feet did.**

**And the father still ran.**

### The Older Son

*"The older brother became angry and refused to go in. So his father went out and pleaded with him... 'My son,' the father said, 'you are always with me, and everything I have is yours'"* (Luke 15:28, 31).

Listen to how the older son speaks in the verses between. He does not say *my brother.* He says **"this son of yours."** He has mentally removed the relationship. And the father, gently, puts it back: **"this brother of yours."**

Here is the heartbreak of the older son. **He was always with the father, but his mind was on his brother.** He had access to everything — *everything I have is yours* — and spent the whole evening outside, doing arithmetic on someone else's portion.

**Mind your plate, not your brother's.** *Focus sa plato mo, hindi sa plato ng bunso.*

### Under the Father's Wings

Put the two sons side by side and you see the whole point. **The younger son lost his mind in a far country. The older son lost his in his father's house.** You do not have to leave to lose your mind. You can stand in the middle of the blessing and still be somewhere else.

**Grace welcomed the younger son home. But what he spent, he did not get back.**

Both sons can receive grace. Both can be saved through the Lord Jesus. **The point is not who gets saved.** The point is the chains you never had to carry in the first place.

**Grace is for both sons, but staying under the Father's wings spares you the chains.**

And let us guard that properly — grace is never permission to keep sinning. *"What shall we say, then? Shall we go on sinning so that grace may increase? By no means!"* (Romans 6:1–2).

*Mind it for free?* — **Free your mind!**

## 4 · The Blessing — Wisdom

*Karunungan.* Start with a question: **what does a blessing look like to you?** *Pera? Promotion? Bahay?*

Here is the correction: **money is not the blessing. Wisdom is the blessing, and wisdom unlocks the reward.** The blessing was never the money itself — it is **the wisdom to make it, to keep it, and to use it.** Money without wisdom leaves as fast as it came.

*"Blessed are those who find wisdom, those who gain understanding... Long life is in her right hand; in her left hand are riches and honor"* (Proverbs 3:13, 16).

Notice the arrangement. **Wisdom is what you find. Riches and honor are in her hand.** They come attached to her; they are not the thing you go looking for.

Solomon understood this. *"So give your servant a discerning heart to govern your people and to distinguish between right and wrong. For who is able to govern this great people of yours?"* And God answered: *"Moreover, I will give you what you have not asked for — both wealth and honor — so that in your lifetime you will have no equal among kings"* (1 Kings 3:9, 13).

**Solomon asked for wisdom first. The rewards followed.**

### How Do We Know It Is a Reward from Blessing?

**It comes through progress, not in a single moment.** Health, wealth, whatever it is — **to be blessed is to be worked upon.** *Hindi instant. Proseso.*

*"Consider it pure joy, my brothers and sisters, whenever you face trials of many kinds, because you know that the testing of your faith produces perseverance. Let perseverance finish its work so that you may be mature and complete, not lacking anything"* (James 1:2–4).

**Let perseverance finish its work.** That is exactly what being worked upon means. Not a lightning strike of blessing — a long, patient forming.

### Outside the Comfort Zone

And here is where it gets uncomfortable. **Wisdom grows outside your comfort zone. Experience needs risk.** You cannot gain the wisdom of a trial you never entered.

**And that is precisely where the devil comes, and where the mind plays.** In the uncertainty. In the change. In the space where you do not yet know how it ends.

So the choice returns: **Mind it for free** — let change and uncertainty dictate my mind. Or **free your mind** — stay free and rely on the Word of God.

*Mind it for free?* — **Free your mind!**

### Ask, and Do Not Be Double-Minded

*"If any of you lacks wisdom, you should ask God, who gives generously to all without finding fault, and it will be given to you... Such a person is double-minded and unstable in all they do"* (James 1:5, 8).

Look at where that sits. Verse 4 ends with *not lacking anything* — and verse 5 opens with *if any of you lacks wisdom.* **Trials and wisdom sit side by side in the same breath.** The trial is the classroom; the wisdom is what you ask for inside it.

And God's posture is extraordinary: **He gives generously to all without finding fault.** He does not lecture you for needing it.

But there is a condition, and it is about the mind. **A double mind cannot receive what God gives generously.** Not because God withholds — but because a mind pulled in two directions has nowhere for wisdom to land.

**Free your mind to see the challenge clearly. Reflect the Word in the challenge, and the challenge becomes wisdom.**

Think back to David one more time. A king with God's favor, a man after God's own heart — and one minded look later, his wisdom had turned into scheming. Read the Lord's word to him through Nathan: *"Why did you despise the word of the Lord by doing what is evil in his eyes?"* (2 Samuel 12:9). **The blessing was blocked by a mind that was not free.**

When our understanding runs out, we ask for wisdom — **and it lands in a free mind.**

## Free Your Mind

*"Do not conform to the pattern of this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind. Then you will be able to test and approve what God's will is — his good, pleasing and perfect will"* (Romans 12:2).

Notice the order one last time. **Transformation comes by the renewing of the mind** — and only *then* are you able to test and approve what God's will is. A cluttered mind cannot read God's will clearly. A renewed one can.

So here is the whole message in five lines:

**Stop the brewing.** Take the thought captive before desire conceives.
**Stop the multiplying.** One problem is one problem; do not turn it into ten overnight.
**Mind your plate.** Not your brother's.
**Stay under His wings.** Grace will meet you either way, but the chains are optional.
**Receive the wisdom.** Ask, and do not be double-minded.

## Challenge

This week, name what is living rent-free.

Be specific. Not *"I worry a lot"* — name the one thing that has moved in and is paying nothing. The message you reread. The person you keep arguing with in your head. The plate that is not yours that you keep looking at.

Then decide what it is. **Is it brewing?** Take it captive today, before it conceives — do not negotiate with it. **Is it multiplying?** Hand it over in prayer with thanksgiving, and let the peace that transcends understanding stand guard. **Is it your brother's plate?** Look down at your own and thank God for what is on it. **Is it a challenge you are standing outside of?** Ask God for wisdom, and ask with one mind, not two.

And each morning this week, before the noise starts, say Isaiah 26:3 over yourself. **A free mind is not an empty mind — it is a steadfast one.**

**Mind it for free, or free your mind?** *Ikaw ang pipili.*
