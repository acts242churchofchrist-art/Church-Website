> *"And what does the Lord require of you? To act justly and to love mercy and to walk humbly with your God."*
> — Micah 6:8

Part 1 stayed in the temple, with two men praying and only one going home justified. Part 2 walks out of the temple and into the week, because **the humility God accepts is the humility other people can feel.**

## What God Requires

The requirement has not changed. Act justly. Love mercy. **Walk humbly with your God.** And the classroom has not changed either: *"Learn from me, for I am gentle and humble in heart"* (Matthew 11:29). We do not develop humility privately and then display it. We learn it from Christ and it shows up in how we treat the person next to us.

## Walking Humbly Before People

### 1 · We Must Walk Humbly Before Others

Paul is blunt about the alternative. *"Do nothing out of selfish ambition or vain conceit"* (Philippians 2:3). Selfish ambition wants the position. Vain conceit wants the credit. Both are perfectly compatible with a full church calendar.

The replacement is not passivity, it is **direction of attention**: in humility value others above yourselves, *"not looking to your own interests but each of you to the interests of the others"* (Philippians 2:4).

Notice it does not say ignore your own interests. It says do not stop there. **Humility shows up at the exact point where someone else's interest costs you something** and you pay it anyway.

### 2 · Jesus Humbled Himself to Serve Us

Then Paul raises the standard past anything we would have dared: *"In your relationships with one another, have the same mindset as Christ Jesus"* (Philippians 2:5).

He *"did not consider equality with God something to be used to his own advantage"* (Philippians 2:6). Hold that. **He was not pretending to be lower than He was.** He genuinely held the highest position there is, and He declined to leverage it.

*"Rather, he made himself nothing by taking the very nature of a servant"* (Philippians 2:7). And further down still, being found in appearance as a man, *"he humbled himself by becoming obedient to death, even death on a cross"* (Philippians 2:8).

Follow the descent. God, to servant, to man, to death, to the most shameful death available. **The One with every right to be served went furthest down of anyone who has ever lived.** Every claim we protect looks smaller measured against that.

### 3 · We Must Imitate the Humility of Our Lord

*"Follow my example, as I follow the example of Christ"* (1 Corinthians 11:1).

That is a bold sentence, and Paul can only write it because of the second half. He is not offering himself as the original. **He is offering himself as proof that an ordinary person can copy Christ**, which is exactly what makes the next three pictures worth looking at.

## Paul's Example of Humility

Watch where Paul places himself, and watch the direction he moves as he gets older in the faith.

### 4 · The Least of the Apostles

*"I am the least of the apostles"*, Paul says, and he does not even deserve the title, because he persecuted the church of God (1 Corinthians 15:9).

Now set that beside the record. He wrote thirteen letters of the New Testament. He planted churches in Corinth, Ephesus and Thessalonica. He opened Christianity to the Gentiles. He was used by God to raise the dead.

**Any one of those would be enough for most of us to build an identity on.** Paul totalled them up and still put himself last, because he could not forget what he had been forgiven of.

### 5 · The Least of the Saints

The ladder goes down, not up. Not least of the apostles now, but *"the very least of all the saints"* (Ephesians 3:8, ESV), and grace given him anyway to preach the unsearchable riches of Christ.

And this is the man who could list five floggings of forty lashes minus one, three beatings with rods, a stoning, three shipwrecks, a night and a day in the open sea (2 Corinthians 11:24–25). Constant danger from rivers, bandits, his own people, Gentiles, city, country, sea and false believers. Labour, sleeplessness, hunger, thirst, cold and exposure, and *"the pressure of my concern for all the churches"* (2 Corinthians 11:26–28).

**Suffering did not earn him a higher opinion of himself.** It lowered him further, and it made him tender toward the weak: *"Who is weak, and I do not feel weak?"* (2 Corinthians 11:29).

### 6 · The Worst of Sinners

The bottom rung. *"Christ Jesus came into the world to save sinners, of whom I am the worst"* (1 Timothy 1:15). Present tense. Not was the worst, **am the worst**.

And he says why God allowed it: so that in him, the worst of sinners, Christ could display His immense patience *"as an example for those who would believe in him"* (1 Timothy 1:16). **Paul's résumé of sin became a public exhibit of mercy.**

Which is why he kept a short leash on himself to the end: he struck a blow to his body and made it his slave, so that after preaching to others he would not himself be *"disqualified for the prize"* (1 Corinthians 9:26–27). **He never graduated out of needing mercy, and never assumed he was safe.**

## What God Does With the Humble

### 7 · Humility Is Rewarded by God

This is not self-hatred with a religious coat on. God attaches a promise to it.

*"All of you, clothe yourselves with humility toward one another"* (1 Peter 5:5), because **God opposes the proud but shows favor to the humble.** Opposes. That is the word. Pride does not merely fail to impress God, it puts you on the wrong side of Him.

*"Humble yourselves, therefore, under God's mighty hand, that he may lift you up in due time"* (1 Peter 5:6). **We go low. He does the lifting, and He picks the timing.** And the anxiety we would normally manage by defending our own position goes somewhere better: *"Cast all your anxiety on him because he cares for you"* (1 Peter 5:7).

Paul closes the same way. Live a life worthy of the calling you received, *"completely humble and gentle, patient, bearing with one another in love"* (Ephesians 4:2), making every effort to keep the unity of the Spirit through the bond of peace. One body, one Spirit, one hope, **one Lord, one faith, one baptism, one God and Father of all** (Ephesians 4:3–6).

Read that list again. **It is a list of things nobody in the room owns more of than anybody else.** Humility is simply agreeing with it.

## Challenge

This week, find the place where the cost is real.

Pick one person in this church whose interests you have been quietly ranking below your own. A brother you compete with. A sister whose ministry you privately rate. Someone whose opinion you have stopped weighing because you decided long ago you were further along. **Value that person above yourself this week, in something that costs you.** Give them the credit, the preference, the last word, the help they did not ask for.

Then do Paul's exercise honestly. Write down the thing about your walk with God you are most pleased with. Underneath it, write what you were saved out of. **Keep them on the same page**, because the moment the first line stops being read next to the second, we are back in Part 1, standing in the temple listing our fasting.

We did not lower ourselves to get here. **He came all the way down first.**

**To God be the glory!**
