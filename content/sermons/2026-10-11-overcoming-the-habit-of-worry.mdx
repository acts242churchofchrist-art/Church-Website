> *"So do not worry about tomorrow; for tomorrow will worry about itself."*
> — Matthew 6:34 (Amplified)

> *"Kaya nga, huwag ninyong alalahanin ang bukas; sapagkat ang bukas ang mag-aalala sa sarili niya."*
> — Mateo 6:34

Notice what Jesus does not say. He does not say your situation is not serious. He does not say tomorrow holds nothing. He says **tomorrow will handle its own share**, and today already has enough.

## The Three Questions Worry Asks

Read Matthew 6:31 and you find worry is not vague. It is specific, and it is always about supply. *"What are we going to eat?"* *"What are we going to drink?"* *"What are we going to wear?"*

The deck marks the diagnosis beside each line, and it is worth sitting with. Verses 31 and 32 are labelled **fear and ignorance**. Verses 33 and 34 are labelled **unbelief**. That is a hard reading of a familiar passage, and it is the right one. Worry is not a nervous habit we picked up. It is a belief about God that has gone quietly wrong.

*"For your heavenly Father knows that you need them"* (Matthew 6:32). **The Gentiles chase these things because they do not have a Father who knows.** We do.

## 1 · Know Your Enemy

The Greek word matters here. When Jesus says do not worry, the word is **merimnaō**, μεριμνᾶτε. It does not mean to care about something. It carries the sense of being pulled in pieces, **overly concerned, divided, distracted**. The Amplified Bible renders it perpetually uneasy.

That is the enemy. Not concern. Division.

And the enemy is a liar, which can be measured. Penn State researchers Lucas LaFreniere and Michelle Newman took 29 people diagnosed with generalized anxiety disorder, had them record their worries over a ten-day period, and then tracked those same worries across thirty days to see which ones actually happened.

**91.4% of the recorded worries never came true.** The most common result per person was 100%. Of the small remainder that did happen, participants generally found they coped better than they had predicted, or learned something from it.

The scale of the problem is not small either. The World Health Organization counts **470 million people** living with an anxiety disorder, more women than men, with symptoms usually starting in childhood or adolescence. Effective treatment exists, and **only about one in four people receive it**.

Hold those two facts together. Worry is enormously common, genuinely treatable, and **almost always wrong about the future**.

## 2 · Focus More on Positive Concern

Here is the turn that makes this sermon more than a scolding. The same Greek root appears in the New Testament as a good thing.

### The negative use

Martha is *"anxious and troubled about many things"* (Luke 10:41), and the word is merimnaō. In the parable of the sower, *"the cares of the world and the deceitfulness of riches"* (Mark 4:19) choke the word, and the word there is merimna.

**Same root. Pointed at yourself, it strangles.**

### The positive use

Now watch it turn. Paul writes that there should be no division in the body, but that *"the members may have the same care for one another"* (1 Corinthians 12:25). The same word. **Pointed at someone else, it builds.**

The deck lists four shapes this takes.

**Concern for the welfare of others** (1 Corinthians 12:25). **Serving and bearing burdens**: *"let us not grow weary of doing good"* (Galatians 6:9), and as we have opportunity, do good to everyone, especially the household of faith (Galatians 6:10). **Loving in action**: if anyone has material possessions and sees a brother or sister in need and has no pity, how can God's love be in that person? *"Let us not love with words or speech but with actions and in truth"* (1 John 3:17–18). **Compassion for the needy**: *"whoever is generous to the poor lends to the Lord"* (Proverbs 19:17), and do not neglect to do good and to share, *"for such sacrifices are pleasing to God"* (Hebrews 13:16).

The practical counsel underneath all four is simple and slightly surprising. **One of the cures for anxiety is a brother or sister in need and the decision to go and help them.**

## 3 · Pray and Petition

*"Don't worry about anything; instead, pray about everything"* (Philippians 4:6, NLT). Tell God what you need, and thank Him for all He has done.

Then the promise: *"you will experience God's peace, which exceeds anything we can understand"* (Philippians 4:7, NLT), and that peace will guard your heart and mind as you live in Christ Jesus.

**Worry and prayer ask exactly the same question.** What about tomorrow, what about the money, what about the diagnosis. The difference is that one of them is addressed to Someone who can answer, and the other is just the question, circling.

## 4 · Surrender to God All Our Anxieties

*"Casting all your anxieties on him, because he cares for you"* (1 Peter 5:7). Merimna again.

**Casting is a transfer of custody, not a loan.** The thing about worry is that we will happily tell God about it while keeping a copy for ourselves to work on at two in the morning. Peter's word does not allow that. You throw it, and it lands somewhere else.

And the reason given is not that the worry is trivial. It is *"because he cares for you"*. **He takes it because He wants you, not because it does not matter.**

## 5 · Put First God's Kingdom and Righteousness

Back to where we started. Do not be anxious about food, drink or clothing, because your Father already knows. *"But first and most importantly seek (aim at, strive after) His kingdom and His righteousness"* (Matthew 6:33, Amplified), His way of doing and being right, **and all these things will be given to you also**.

Sa Magandang Balita Biblia: *"bigyang-halaga ninyo ang kaharian ng Diyos"* at ang pamumuhay ayon sa kanyang kalooban, at ibibigay niya ang lahat ng kailangan ninyo (Mateo 6:33).

Read the order. **Worry is a priority problem before it is a feeling problem.** Jesus does not tell the anxious person to relax. He tells them to put something else first, and promises the rest follows.

## Action Steps

The sermon closes with three, and they are deliberately stronger than advice.

**Repent of the sin of worry.** The word is **metanoia**, a change of mind, an inner reorientation. Not an apology for being anxious, but a turn.

**Be set free of the spirit of worry.** Name it as something to be released from rather than managed.

**Receive God's faith through grace**, and regularly declare and act in the spirit of victory.

## Challenge

Do the experiment on yourself this week.

Write down every worry as it arrives, with the date. Do not argue with any of it, just record it. **Then put a reminder in your phone for thirty days from now**, and on that day read the list back and mark which ones actually happened. The researchers found 91.4% never did. You will probably find something similar, in your own handwriting, which is much harder to dismiss than a statistic from a pulpit.

Then do the second half, which is the part that actually changes things. **Pick one person in this church who is in real need and take the worry you were going to spend on yourself and spend it on them instead.** Merimnaō pointed outward. Bring the food, make the visit, pay the thing quietly.

And tomorrow morning, before the three questions start, answer them in the right order. **Seek first His kingdom.** The rest has already been promised.

**To God be the glory!**
