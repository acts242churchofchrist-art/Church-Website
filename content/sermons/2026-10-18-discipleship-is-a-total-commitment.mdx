> *"Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit."*
> — Matthew 28:19

> *"Kaya't humayo kayo, gawin ninyong alagad ko ang mga tao sa lahat ng mga bansa."*
> — Mateo 28:19

The last instruction Jesus gave before He left was not make converts, or make attenders, or make a crowd. **Make disciples.** This message asks what that word actually costs, and the honest answer is everything.

## Discipleship Defined

Start with the vocabulary, because we have quietly swapped it. A disciple is a true follower of Jesus. It is simply what the New Testament calls the people we would now call Christians.

The proportion is worth hearing. **The word disciple appears 269 times in the New Testament. The word Christian appears three.** And the first of those three is a label outsiders attached to disciples at Antioch, not a title the church chose for itself.

That is not a word game. It is a warning about what we think we signed up for. **Jesus wants all people to become His disciples in order to be saved**, and He never offered a lesser category.

## The Cost of Being a Disciple

What happens next in Luke 14 is one of the strangest moves in the Gospels. *"Large crowds were traveling with Jesus"* (Luke 14:25). A preacher with a crowd usually works to keep it. **Jesus turned around and made the terms harder.**

## 1 · The Crowds Were Casual Followers, Not Committed Ones

Anyone who comes to Him and does not *"hate father and mother, wife and children"*, and even their own life, *"cannot be my disciple"* (Luke 14:26).

The word hate here is the Hebrew way of stating a comparison in the strongest terms, and the comparison is the point: **nothing and nobody outranks Him, including you.** Note who He said it to. Not opponents. Not the uncommitted standing at a distance. He said it to the people already walking along with Him.

**Proximity is not commitment.** You can travel with the crowd for years.

## 2 · Carrying Our Cross Means Total Obedience and Sacrifice

*"And whoever does not carry their cross and follow me cannot be my disciple"* (Luke 14:27).

We have softened this one almost beyond recognition. A difficult relative is not a cross. A health problem is not a cross. **To the people listening, a cross was what a condemned man carried to the place of his execution**, in public, through a jeering street.

A cross is not a hardship that happens to you. **It is something you pick up on purpose**, the way our Lord did.

## 3 · There Is No Turning Back

Then the first picture. *"Suppose one of you wants to build a tower. Won't you first sit down and estimate the cost?"* (Luke 14:28). Because if the foundation goes in and the money runs out, everyone who walks past will say: *"This person began to build and wasn't able to finish"* (Luke 14:30).

Read what the ridicule is actually aimed at. **Not the smallness of the tower. The abandonment of it.** Jesus is not warning us away from starting. He is warning us against starting without having counted, because following Him is a lifetime commitment and the unfinished version is visible to everybody.

## 4 · Discipleship Is Total Surrender to Jesus

The second picture. A king with ten thousand men considers an enemy marching with twenty thousand, and if he cannot win he sends a delegation to ask for terms of peace while the other is still far off (Luke 14:31–32).

Then the line the whole sermon is named after: *"those of you who do not give up everything you have cannot be my disciples"* (Luke 14:33).

The king's strength was honestly assessed and found insufficient. **That is the right conclusion about ourselves too.** Surrender here is not a mood. It is handing over the inventory, and Jesus names the total: everything you have.

## 5 · A Christian's Life Must Have a Useful Purpose

*"Salt is good, but if it loses its saltiness, how can it be made salty again?"* (Luke 14:34). It is no use for the soil and no use even on the manure pile. It gets thrown out (Luke 14:35).

Notice that salt is not used up by being useful. **It fails by becoming nothing in particular.** A disciple who costs nothing, risks nothing and changes nothing has not been spent in service. They have simply lost the thing that made them worth having in the kitchen.

**We are called for a purpose**, and the purpose is the whole reason we were called.

## 6 · We Are Reminded to Listen and Respond

*"Whoever has ears to hear, let them hear"* (Luke 14:35).

He does not end with a technique or a program. After the hardest terms He ever put to a crowd, the closing request is simply that we do not look away from them. **Let this be a reminder of our identity.** We are disciples, or we are part of a crowd that traveled with Him for a while.

## Challenge

Three things, and they are deliberately hard.

**Discipleship is not optional for those who want to be saved.** It is mandatory. Settle that first, because everything else in this message depends on it being true.

**Jesus is calling devoted men and women to join Him in the difficult work of building His church.** Difficult is His word for it, not ours. Choose the part of that work you have been avoiding because it is inconvenient, and take it on this week.

**Be willing to pay any price to become what He has called you to be, and to follow through on what He has told you to do.** This week, do the counting Jesus actually asked for. Sit down, like the tower builder and the king, and write out what following Him is currently costing you and what it would cost to finish. If the honest total frightens you, you have understood the passage.

Discipleship is a high calling with a high reward.

**To God be the glory!**
