> *"Therefore go and make disciples of all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father and of the Son and of the Holy Spirit."* — Matthew 28:19

> *"Kaya't humayo kayo, gawin ninyong alagad ko ang mga tao sa lahat ng mga bansa."* — Mateo 28:19

Last Sunday our visiting preacher, Bro. Tony, spoke about the habit of worry. When Bro. Marc first saw the slides, he honestly thought the church did not need that message — we had been taught for years not to worry. Then Bro. Tony asked who had worried at least once that week, and hands went up all over the room.

The point is not to put anyone down. **It is that a message only works when it is heard, understood, and lived.** So listen to this one that way.

A church went on an outing, everyone bringing food to share. A pig and a chicken wandered past and decided they should contribute too. *What should we bring?* asked the pig. The chicken had the answer: **ham and eggs.** The pig thought about it and complained. *Wait — for you, that's a contribution. For me, that's total commitment.*

That is the whole message. **Discipleship is not contributing something. It is giving yourself.**

The last instruction Jesus gave before He went back to the Father was not make converts, or make attenders, or make a crowd. **Make disciples.** This message asks what that word actually costs, and the honest answer is everything.

## Discipleship Defined

Why did Jesus want people from every nation to become His disciples? Was it so all our requests would be granted? So we would never be tested? So we could live however we like? No. **So that they would be saved** — so they could enter the kingdom of heaven. Which means that a person who will not become a disciple of Christ cannot enter God's kingdom. Settle that first, because everything else rests on it.

Now the vocabulary, because we have quietly swapped it. A disciple is a true follower of Jesus. It is simply what the New Testament calls the people we would now call Christians.

The proportion is worth hearing. **The word disciple appears 269 times in the New Testament. The word Christian appears three.** And the first of those three is a label outsiders attached to disciples at Antioch, not a title the church chose for itself.

That is not a word game. It is a warning about what we think we signed up for. The word *Christian* has been watered down. Too often the world cannot tell who the Christians are, because their lifestyle looks the same — the wild parties, the places a believer should not be seen, the lies told without even blinking. If you are not used to lying, your eyes give you away. Some Christians lie with their eyes wide open.

**Jesus wants all people to become His disciples in order to be saved**, and He never offered a lesser category. This is really a review of our first principles — so we are reminded.

## The Cost of Being a Disciple

What happens next in Luke 14 is one of the strangest moves in the Gospels. *"Large crowds were traveling with Jesus"* (Luke 14:25). He had fed thousands with a few loaves and fish, and He was healing the sick, so of course the crowds grew. A preacher with a crowd usually works to keep it. **Jesus turned around and made the terms harder.**

## 1 · The Crowds Were Casual Followers, Not Committed Ones

Anyone who comes to Him and does not *"hate father and mother, wife and children"*, and even their own life, *"cannot be my disciple"* (Luke 14:26).

So, a show of hands. Who here is married — and do you love your spouse? Who has brothers and sisters — and do you love them? *Meron yatang hindi.* Who has children? Do you love yourself? Of course we do. That is exactly the list Jesus names.

The word hate here is the Hebrew way of stating a comparison in the strongest terms, and the comparison is the point: **nothing and nobody outranks Him, including you.** If you examine your heart and find you love your child more than Jesus, your girlfriend or your spouse more than Jesus, then by His own words you cannot be His disciple.

When Bro. Marc came home from Saudi Arabia as a new believer, he told his wife plainly that he loved Jesus more than her now. She thought he had lost his mind. **He had — he was crazy in love with Jesus**, more than his own life.

Note who Jesus said it to. Not opponents. Not the uncommitted standing at a distance. **He said it to the people already walking along with Him.** They were not against Him. They were after what they could get from Him.

**Proximity is not commitment.** You can travel with the crowd for years — like the chicken, giving only what is convenient. The pig gives the ham, and he does not walk away from it.

Sometimes we shrink the meaning of being a disciple: *I accepted Jesus, so I'm saved. Once saved, always saved — I can do what I want now; a gift can't be taken back.* Read again what Jesus actually said. **If you do not love Him above everything, you cannot be His disciple.** It is not a hobby, something we do when we feel like worshiping. The Father gave His only Son. What do we give — whatever coin we happen to pull out of our pocket?

Bro. Marc does not want Jesus to return and have anyone say, *nobody taught us properly.* The Lord could come at any time to gather His true followers. **Jesus is not after numbers.** When He gave a hard teaching and the crowds walked away, only the Twelve remained — and He let them choose whether to stay too. He was looking for committed followers, not casual ones.

## 2 · Carrying Our Cross Means Total Obedience and Sacrifice

*"And whoever does not carry their cross and follow me cannot be my disciple"* (Luke 14:27). And *whoever* means no one is exempt.

We have softened this one almost beyond recognition. A difficult relative is not a cross. A health problem is not a cross. **To the people listening, a cross was what a condemned man carried to the place of his execution**, in public, through a jeering street.

The cross stands for **obedience and sacrifice.** As the communion message reminded us this morning, Jesus prayed for the cup to pass from Him if possible — *yet not my will, but Yours.* He obeyed the Father all the way to Calvary and endured the pain, the insults and the shame so that our sins could be forgiven.

So are we carrying ours? Do we feel the cost of being a Christian, or does nothing change? Forgiving an enemy is a cross. *He cheated me, hurt me, slandered me, cursed me, insulted me* — **forgive him anyway.** It is hard. Jesus' road was harder, and He obeyed.

A cross is not a hardship that happens to you. **It is something you pick up on purpose**, the way our Lord did.

## 3 · There Is No Turning Back

Then the first picture. *"Suppose one of you wants to build a tower. Won't you first sit down and estimate the cost?"* (Luke 14:28). Because if the foundation goes in and the money runs out, everyone who walks past will say: *"This person began to build and wasn't able to finish"* (Luke 14:30).

We have all seen a half-built house and thought: *didn't they even budget for it? Sayang.*

Bro. Marc counted the cost before he was baptized. His job required entertaining his bosses at night, keeping them company so he could win their favor and get promoted. Could he walk away from that? One week after his baptism, the boss arrived — and he could not say no. He went to the brother who had studied the Bible with him: *Bro, I couldn't say no.* They prayed. The next time, it was not only his boss but managers from all over the provinces. **By God's grace, he found the courage to decline:** *Boss, pasensya na, hindi na ako makakasama.*

Then there is the man who wanted a tattoo that said *I LOVE DONNA* for two thousand pesos, and only found out at the end that all the colors brought it to two thousand five hundred. He had exactly two thousand. He walked out with *I LOVE DON* — and a very confused wife. **Count the cost before you start.**

Read what the ridicule is actually aimed at. **Not the smallness of the tower. The abandonment of it.** Jesus is not warning us away from starting. He is warning us against starting without having counted, because following Him is a lifetime commitment, and the one who stands firm to the end will be saved (Matthew 24:13).

And when you grow weak — you will — **reach for your brothers and sisters.** We are one text away. *Bro. Marc, Sis. Normie, can we have coffee?* Confess your sins to one another and pray for each other so that you may be healed (James 5:16). Find a trusted brother or sister who keeps you accountable. There is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus (Romans 8:1). The Lord knows our weakness. **What matters is that you are still fighting.**

The danger is when you stop asking for help, until your heart grows calloused. Nobody gives up on discipleship in one instant. **It happens a step at a time.** You stop attending. You stop reading. You stop praying. You hardly notice — until the enemy has a foothold in your life.

## 4 · Discipleship Is Total Surrender to Jesus

The second picture. A king with ten thousand men considers an enemy marching with twenty thousand, and if he cannot win he sends a delegation to ask for terms of peace while the other is still far off (Luke 14:31–32).

Who wins, ten thousand or twenty thousand? Obviously the twenty thousand. **The larger army is God; the smaller one is us.** If you know you cannot win against God, why keep rebelling against Him? Ask for terms **while He is still far off** — not when the army is already at your walls. Too many people wait for their final illness to surrender, and some die before they ever get the chance.

Surrender while you are young, while you still have time to serve. Bro. Marc remembers being in college when the pastor invited people to come forward and receive Jesus. He wanted to go — but he thought, *if I go up there, no more nightlife; I'm still enjoying myself.* Then came two near-death experiences, and both times he knew where he would go if he died. He asked Jesus, *is there still hope for me?* He was working for a company where the good times were part of the job. **God not only saved him; He gave him the chance to serve.**

Then the line the whole sermon is named after: *"those of you who do not give up everything you have cannot be my disciples"* (Luke 14:33).

A king who surrenders is no longer king. Whatever the greater king commands, he obeys. **When we surrender to Jesus, what He wants, we do; what He forbids, we leave.** That is why sin is rebellion — refusing to obey the King. Let us not be rebellious disciples.

The king's strength was honestly assessed and found insufficient. **That is the right conclusion about ourselves too.** Surrender here is not a mood. It is handing over the inventory, and Jesus names the total: everything you have.

## 5 · A Christian's Life Must Have a Useful Purpose

*"Salt is good, but if it loses its saltiness, how can it be made salty again?"* (Luke 14:34). It is no use for the soil and no use even on the manure pile. It gets thrown out (Luke 14:35).

Salt preserves. **We are the salt of the earth**, holding back the decay of the world. Bro. Marc taught that this is why the Antichrist has not yet been revealed: the church is still here.

And salvation is a gift we must guard. Imagine a wealthy sponsor offers to fund your entire expedition to Mount Everest. The money is a gift — **but you still have to climb.**

Here is how saltiness gets lost. You are newly hired, and the office goes out drinking. You cannot say no, so you join in — and now you want to invite them to church. *Wait, this guy's a Christian? He drinks more than I do.* **Your testimony is gone**, and you cannot easily explain it back.

Notice that salt is not used up by being useful. **It fails by becoming nothing in particular.** A disciple who costs nothing, risks nothing and changes nothing has not been spent in service. We are not decorations in the church. Give your time, your talent and your treasure, and live a life with a good testimony.

**We are called for a purpose**, and the purpose is the whole reason we were called.

## 6 · We Are Reminded to Listen and Respond

*"Whoever has ears to hear, let them hear"* (Luke 14:35).

We all have ears. **Hear this message, understand it, and then share it** — with friends, relatives, officemates and classmates who do not yet have a relationship with the Lord. Study the Bible with them.

He does not end with a technique or a program. After the hardest terms He ever put to a crowd, the closing request is simply that we do not look away from them. **Let this be a reminder of our identity.** We are disciples, or we are part of a crowd that traveled with Him for a while.

## Challenge

Three things, and they are deliberately hard.

**Discipleship is not optional for those who want to be saved.** It is mandatory. Jesus warned that some will say *Lord, Lord, didn't we prophesy in Your name and perform miracles?* and He will answer, *I never knew you. Away from me* (Matthew 7:21–23). They contributed. They never committed.

**Jesus is calling devoted men and women to join Him in the difficult work of building His church.** We are His body — His hands. He said we would do even greater things than He did (John 14:12); He ministered for three years, and we have a lifetime. So it starts at home. If you shout and curse in your house, how will you invite the people you live with? When Bro. Marc walked into the office smiling the Monday after his baptism, his co-workers had a theory: *maybe his wife didn't scold him today.* **People should notice the change.** If anyone is in Christ, the new creation has come (2 Corinthians 5:17).

**Be willing to pay any price to become what He has called you to be, and to follow through on what He has told you to do.** The price is different for each of us — often it is simply our time. This week, do the counting Jesus actually asked for. Sit down, like the tower builder and the king, and write out what following Him is currently costing you and what it would cost to finish. If the honest total frightens you, you have understood the passage.

And as the welcome verse reminded us this morning, in all things God works for the good of those who love Him and are called according to His purpose (Romans 8:28). Bro. Marc has seen Him turn his own family's hardest season around. **Love Him, live for His purpose, and He can turn it around for you.**

Discipleship is a high calling with a high reward.

**To God be the glory!**
