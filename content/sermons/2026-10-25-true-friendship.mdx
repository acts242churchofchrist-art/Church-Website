> *"A friend loves at all times, and a brother is born for a time of adversity."* — Proverbs 17:17

Three friends were shipwrecked and stranded on a deserted island. One day a genie appeared and granted each of them one wish. The first friend missed his family: *I wish I were home.* And he was gone. The second missed the girl he loved: *I wish I were with her.* Gone too. The third looked around the empty beach and felt very alone. *I wish my two friends were back here with me.*

So they lived happily ever after — together, on the island.

We laughed, but friendship is exactly that: **sticking together through thick and thin** — *sa hirap at ginhawa.*

True friendship is a divine design, modeled finally by Jesus Christ, who **initiated the relationship with us and then laid down His life for His friends**. This message takes it in three parts: why God built us for it, what a faithful friend actually does, and the friend who outlasts all the others.

## 1 · The Design and Value of True Friendship

### It was never good to be alone

The first thing God called *not good* was not sin. It was solitude. *"It is not good for the man to be alone"* (Genesis 2:18), said over a world He had already called good seven times. So He made Adam a partner. **Connection was designed in, not added later.**

Ecclesiastes puts the practical case. Two are better than one, because they have a good return for their labor. If either of them falls, one can help the other up, *"but pity anyone who falls and has no one to help them up"* (Ecclesiastes 4:10). Two lying together keep warm. And *"a cord of three strands is not quickly broken"* (Ecclesiastes 4:12).

Bro. James learned the first half of that the hard way. In college, his thesis group was made up of his friends — and every one of them was lazy. They were absent again and again. He did the research; at meetings, nobody contributed. It got bad enough that the professors had to step in. **Doing group work alone is crushing**, and when one member fails, the whole group fails with him. United we stand, divided we fall — in a company, on a sports team, in a school project, and most of all in a church.

Read that last line of Ecclesiastes carefully. **It is three strands, not two.** The strongest friendships in Scripture are not two people facing each other. They are two people with God woven through.

True friendship doubles our joys — *kain tayo sa labas, libre kita* — and halves our sorrows. It gives honest counsel. And it sharpens our character.

### Iron sharpens iron

*"As iron sharpens iron, so one person sharpens another"* (Proverbs 27:17). It means we bring out the best in each other, instead of dragging our friends down.

Think of how a blade is forged. It is heated, then hammered. If that were you, it would hurt. **But the finished blade is beautiful because it went through the fire and the hammer.** If you have a friend who sharpens you into a better person, be grateful for that friend.

Four things are packed into that picture, and they are worth separating.

**Mutual growth.** Sharpening is not one-way. Both blades are changed by the contact, which means a friendship where only one person does the correcting is not this. Bro. James remembers a season when friends were pulling him into gambling at the casino. His mother warned him: *if they were true friends, they would not bring you there.* He was stubborn and went anyway — and lost five thousand pesos that day. **If only he had listened.**

**Constructive friction.** Blades are not sharpened by agreement. They are sharpened by resistance. **Real friendship involves hard truths and accountability**, not comfortable nodding. If your brother or sister is falling into sin, do not say *okay lang yan.* Real-talk them — not to hurt or shame them, but because you love them. That is what Jesus did with His disciples whenever they fell short. Bro. James says he knows his wife loves him, because she is always correcting him. **It is not nagging. It is love.**

We all have pride, and there are days we do not want anyone's unsolicited opinion. So we get offended, and the relationship breaks. **It all comes down to pride.** Which matters more, your pride or the person you love? Jesus is God, and He chose His relationship with you over His pride. If Bro. James had that kind of power, he admits, one flick of his finger and the people who once hurt him would have vanished like bubbles. **Thank God, God is not like us.**

**Removing dullness.** A neglected blade goes blunt simply by being left alone — then it rusts. Isolation and unchallenging company do the same thing to a person, slowly, without anything dramatic happening. With no one to correct you, you stop noticing that you are wrong, or that you are already hurting people.

**Trust and intention.** This only works inside committed relationships where the person giving the feedback genuinely cares — the way our elders love each member of this church, the way good parents love their children, the way a spouse loves. The same words from someone who does not love you are just criticism.

Which is why Proverbs is so blunt elsewhere: whoever loves discipline loves knowledge, *"but whoever hates correction is stupid"* (Proverbs 12:1).

Bro. James confesses he has been that kind of stupid many times. When his wife corrected his driving, his first thought used to be, *you don't even know how to drive — why are you correcting me?* He prayed about it, and now he listens without arguing, because the alternative might be a crash. In school, when a groupmate tried to correct him, his attitude was *I'm the one doing all the work here.* They failed. He hated being corrected by anyone younger. **He was a very prideful man, and it cost him many failures, even at home.** Then God changed his heart. If God could do that with someone as proud as him, how much more with you?

**The willingness to be corrected is the entry fee for friendship that actually changes you.**

## 2 · The Marks of a Faithful Friend

### Presence

*"A friend loves at all times, and a brother is born for a time of adversity"* (Proverbs 17:17). Not loves when convenient. **At all times**, with the adversity clause written in specifically.

What kind of friend says *I'm here for you* and then cannot be reached when you are the one with the problem? You message, *Bro, I need someone to talk to, I'm going through something big* — and they leave you on seen. **Is that a true friend?**

This is why we have **discipling time** in this church — one-on-ones with our elders and leaders, sharing what we are going through, correcting what needs correcting. That is the brother born for adversity, built into the life of the church.

And Proverbs is practical about proximity: do not forsake your friend or your family's friend, because *"better a neighbor nearby than a relative far away"* (Proverbs 27:10). **Showing up physically still counts for something no message can replace.**

### Gossip destroys friendship

This is where the message spends the most time, and rightly, because this is the sin that quietly dismantles a church family.

Bro. James was honest: *chismis is life.* He does not spread gossip, he says, but he loves to listen to it. Somehow it is delicious. Maybe it is just our nature.

*"A gossip betrays a confidence, but a trustworthy person keeps a secret"* (Proverbs 11:13). *"A gossip separates close friends"* (Proverbs 16:28). The words of a gossip are like choice morsels, *"they go down to the inmost parts"* (Proverbs 18:8), which is exactly why we enjoy them. Without wood a fire goes out, and *"without a gossip a quarrel dies down"* (Proverbs 26:20). And the instruction is not to manage it but to leave: a gossip betrays a confidence, *"so avoid anyone who talks too much"* (Proverbs 20:19).

You know how it works among friends: **whoever is absent becomes the topic.** So never miss church, or we will talk about you — *joke lang.*

But watch what it does. Say a friend tells others that some girl has been chasing Bro. James because he is so handsome. Someone outside the circle repeats it to him. Now his wife is upset, he confronts his friend — *I trusted you* — and the friendship is broken over a story that was never true. And every time it is passed on, it changes a little, until nobody knows what really happened. **Would you ever trust a gossip with your own problems?** Once trust cracks, it is almost impossible to restore.

**Other people's lives are not entertainment.** Every person is fighting their own battles. How would you feel if someone gossiped about what you are going through?

The New Testament does not soften it. *"Do not let any unwholesome talk come out of your mouths"*, only what builds others up according to their needs (Ephesians 4:29). That is iron sharpening iron. Paul lists gossips in the same breath as envy, strife, deceit and malice (Romans 1:29). And Timothy is warned about those who go from house to house and become *"busybodies who talk nonsense"* (1 Timothy 5:13).

It is not easy. But we are disciples of Jesus Christ, and **the way we live outside these walls is what tells the world we are His.** Can your friend believe you are a Christian if you are the biggest gossip they know?

**Every friendship in this room is only as safe as the last thing you repeated about someone.**

### Guarding the tongue

The remedy in Scripture is not better intentions. It is a guard.

*"Keep your tongue from evil and your lips from telling lies"* (Psalm 34:13). And David's prayer, which is the honest one: *"Set a guard over my mouth, LORD; keep watch over the door of my lips"* (Psalm 141:3). **He asked God to post the sentry, because he knew he could not be trusted with the door himself.**

James makes it a family matter: *"Brothers and sisters, do not slander one another"* (James 4:11). Not strangers. One another.

Once a word is out, you cannot take it back. And guarding your tongue starts with guarding your heart, because the mouth speaks what the heart is full of (Luke 6:45). In a discipling group, someone may confess a sin they are struggling with, asking prayer for forgiveness. **What is shared in that circle stays in that circle.** Imagine finding out that what you confessed is now being discussed by people who were never in the room.

### Long-term support

Then the example. After Damascus, Saul came to Jerusalem and tried to join the disciples, *"but they were all afraid of him, not believing that he really was a disciple"* (Acts 9:26). Every one of them had a reason. He had been hunting them.

Picture it in our own terms. Suppose Bro. James had spent years persecuting this church — going after the brothers who preach — and then was suddenly baptized. Would you welcome him right away? That is exactly what Saul faced.

*"But Barnabas took him and brought him to the apostles"* (Acts 9:27), and told them what had happened on the road and how Saul had preached fearlessly in Damascus. After that, Saul stayed with them and moved about freely in Jerusalem (Acts 9:28).

Sit with how much that cost. **Barnabas put his own standing behind a man everyone else considered dangerous.** Half the New Testament exists downstream of one friend being willing to vouch. Could you vouch for a friend with a past, and say with confidence, *this person has been changed by the Lord Jesus Christ*?

## 3 · The Ultimate Friend: Jesus

Human friendship has a ceiling, and Proverbs names it. *"One who has unreliable friends soon comes to ruin, but there is a friend who sticks closer than a brother"* (Proverbs 18:24).

Most of us have had friends who were there for the good times and vanished when things got hard. Bro. James chose his friends carefully. His siblings tease him for being *the person with no friends*; he was an introvert who tired quickly in big gatherings. But the few he has were the ones who were real with him. **Few but real. Quality over quantity.** Treasure friends like that, especially the ones who stayed through the worst of what you went through.

*"Greater love has no one than this: to lay down one's life for one's friends"* (John 15:13). Most of us will never be asked to die for a friend. But can you give your time, your attention, your presence when they are struggling? **Can you keep their secret when they open up to you?**

**Initiative.** He did not wait for us to clean ourselves up. *"I no longer call you servants, because a servant does not know his master's business. Instead, I have called you friends"* (John 15:15), because everything He learned from the Father He has made known to us. **That is a change of status, not a change of mood.** God stepped across the gap between Creator and creation and did not treat us as distant workers. It is like a wealthy man stepping down to befriend someone poor, because that person is precious to him.

**Sacrifice.** He said *greater love has no one than this* on the night He was betrayed, and the measure of the friendship was taken the next afternoon. No human friend can give what He gave.

**Unfailing presence.** Human friendships fade through distance, time and death. His does not. *"And surely I am with you always, to the very end of the age"* (Matthew 28:20). They are His last words in Matthew's Gospel, which is the point: He was leaving physically and promising to remain actively present through His Spirit for the whole of human history.

And because we want our friends to be saved too, being a true friend means going and making disciples, baptizing them and teaching them to obey everything He commanded (Matthew 28:19–20).

**He will not misunderstand you, disappoint you, or leave.**

## Challenge

Three steps, and the order matters.

**Evaluate your circle.** Who in your life actually loves you, points you closer to Jesus, and prays for you honestly? Write the names. If the list is short, that is information, not a verdict. And if you are going to stake your life on anyone, stake it on the sure win.

**Be a friend first.** Stop waiting for someone to reciprocate. Pick one person in this congregation who is on the outside of things and go to them this week. Somebody did this for Saul when nobody else would.

**Rest in Christ.** Before you go looking for the perfect human best friend, anchor yourself in the friendship that has already been proved. The others will disappoint you eventually. He has already laid down His life, which settles the question of where you stand.

And one practical test for the week. **Before you repeat anything about anyone, ask whether you are building them up according to their needs.** If not, let the quarrel die for lack of wood.

**To God be the glory!**
