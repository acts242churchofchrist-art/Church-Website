> *"But he knows the way that I take; when he has tested me, I will come forth as gold."*
> — Job 23:10

That sentence was spoken by a man who had lost his children, his health and his property, and who was at that moment unable to find God anywhere. **He is not describing a feeling.** He is describing what he believes is happening to him while he cannot feel anything at all.

## Natural and Supernatural Reactions

Start with the honest list. When a trial lands, what actually shows up is **frustration, worry, complaining and anger.** None of that has to be learned. It arrives by itself, immediately, and usually before we have decided anything.

Then the message sets one word beside that list, with a question mark attached.

**Joyful?**

The question mark is doing real work. It is not pretending that joy is the natural response. It is asking whether we believe a different response is even available.

## 1 · Joy Is Our Supernatural Reaction to Trials

*"Consider it pure joy, my brothers and sisters, whenever you face trials of many kinds"* (James 1:2).

Read it carefully, because it is easily misheard. James does not say feel happy about the trial. He says **consider**, which is an act of judgment rather than an emotion. And he says *whenever*, not *if*.

So how is that possible? The message gives three answers, and all three matter.

**It is made possible by the Holy Spirit**, who enables us to see the situation and respond to it from God's point of view rather than from inside our own panic.

**It is a conscious choice**, and one we have to make again and again, because we are going to face trials again and again. There is no single decision that settles this permanently.

**We must not trust our feelings.** That is the hardest of the three and the most practical. Feelings in a trial are real information about us and poor information about God.

Then the preacher's own advice, which is the line most people will remember: **when trials come, be a student, not a victim.** The victim's question is *why did this happen to me?* The student's question is *what can I learn from this?* The circumstances are identical. The outcome is not.

## 2 · The Testing of Our Faith Has a Purpose

*"Because you know that the testing of your faith produces perseverance"* (James 1:3).

**How do we know?** Not because experience proves it, and not because it feels true in the middle. We know it because God said it. In a trial, that may be the only ground available.

The word **testing** here refers to the process by which gold is purified. Not destruction. Refining. Heat applied deliberately, for long enough, to separate what is valuable from what is not.

Which is exactly Job's confidence: *"when he has tested me, I will come forth as gold"* (Job 23:10). He does not say if, and he does not say I hope.

And the implication is uncomfortable. **Until your faith is tested, it stays theoretical.** Until it has had weight put on it, it remains a speculation, a thing you believe you would do rather than a thing you have done.

## 3 · Perseverance Is Only Developed in Hard Times

God uses trials to produce endurance, which the English versions also render as perseverance, steadfastness or patience.

Revelation puts it where the stakes are highest, among Christians refusing to receive the mark of the beast: *"This calls for patient endurance on the part of the people of God who keep his commands and remain faithful to Jesus"* (Revelation 14:12).

In the early church the martyrs gained the respect of unbelievers for exactly this quality. At the moment of death, **they still had it. They died with their faith intact.**

Then the observation that closes the point. **Perseverance is not tested in the good times.** It can only be exercised and developed in the hard ones, which means a life with no pressure in it will never produce this, no matter how sincere.

## 4 · The Trials We Face Are for Our Benefit

*"Let perseverance finish its work so that you may be mature and complete, not lacking anything"* (James 1:4).

Note **finish**. The work is interrupted when we quit early, and most of us want the maturity without the duration.

The message is honest about the danger here: **we may not see God's love while we are going through it.** That is not framed as a failure of faith. It is named as the normal difficulty.

What remains in our hands is narrow but real. **We cannot choose our trials. We can choose how we respond to them**, and it is our faith and our perspective that make the difference.

**God does not intend to destroy us** by the trials He allows to come our way. The things that are so painful now will one day be seen as benefits to our spiritual growth. So we should not complain when hard times come, but rejoice, if we believe God.

**Every trial is another step on the stairway** that leads from immaturity to maturity, and from earth to heaven.

## The Commitment

The sermon ends by asking for a decision rather than agreement.

Do we want to go through the process that leads to maturity and completeness? If the answer is yes, it takes a commitment, and the message spells it out:

**I will not give up, no matter what happens or how bad life may be. I will hold on and trust God, because He loves me and the Lord has something in store for me.**

## Challenge

**Make the decision now, while nothing is on fire.** You will not be deciding calmly in the middle of it. Say the commitment above out loud this week, before you need it.

**Then change the question.** Whatever you are currently carrying, you have been asking why it happened to you. Ask the student's question instead and write the answer down: what can I learn from this? Not what it means cosmically. What you are actually learning, this month, that you would not have learned otherwise.

**And if you cannot see God's love in it right now, say so honestly rather than performing joy.** Job could not see Him either, and the verse this message is built on was spoken from exactly that position. **He knows the way that I take.** That is a statement about God's knowledge, not about your visibility, and it is true on the days you feel nothing.

**To God be the glory!**
